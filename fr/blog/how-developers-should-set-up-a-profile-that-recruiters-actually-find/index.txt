e:Tce4a,<p>Most developers treat their professional profile like a form they are required to complete.</p>
<p>They paste in parts of their résumé, add a list of technologies, select a current job title, and leave the page untouched until the next job search begins.</p>
<p>That approach misses what a professional profile is designed to do.</p>
<p>A résumé is usually reviewed after someone has already received it.</p>
<p>A searchable profile must complete two earlier tasks:</p>
<ol>
<li>Appear in the right searches.</li>
<li>Convince someone to keep reading after a brief skim.</li>
</ol>
<p>A profile can fail before anyone reaches your experience section.</p>
<p>You may be an excellent backend engineer, but if your profile never contains the phrases recruiters search for, you may not appear in their results.</p>
<p>You may have meaningful technical achievements, but if your headline says only “Software Engineer at Acme,” the person scanning results may never open your profile.</p>
<p>A strong developer profile should help the right reader understand, quickly:</p>
<ul>
<li>what kind of developer you are;</li>
<li>which technologies you use professionally;</li>
<li>what level of responsibility you have;</li>
<li>which problems you solve well;</li>
<li>what you have built or improved;</li>
<li>where you can work;</li>
<li>what kind of role you want next;</li>
<li>how to contact you.</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal is not to attract every recruiter.</p>
<p>It is to become easier to find by the companies and hiring teams that are relevant to you.</p>
<h1>Understand how recruiters search for developers</h1>
<p>Recruiters and hiring managers often begin with a search query rather than a name.</p>
<p>They may search for combinations such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Senior Backend Engineer PostgreSQL</li>
<li>React TypeScript remote</li>
<li>Staff Platform Engineer Kubernetes</li>
<li>iOS Swift SwiftUI</li>
<li>Python data engineer Airflow</li>
<li>Rust distributed systems</li>
<li>Frontend accessibility design systems</li>
<li>Android Kotlin Jetpack Compose</li>
<li>Node.js GraphQL AWS</li>
<li>Security engineer application security</li>
</ul>
<p>They may then narrow results by:</p>
<ul>
<li>location;</li>
<li>time zone;</li>
<li>seniority;</li>
<li>years of experience;</li>
<li>current or previous title;</li>
<li>availability;</li>
<li>employment preference;</li>
<li>industry;</li>
<li>language;</li>
<li>work authorization.</li>
</ul>
<p>If your profile does not contain the terms that accurately describe your experience, you may not appear in the search at all.</p>
<p>That does not mean you should fill the page with repeated keywords.</p>
<p>It means your professional identity should be expressed in the language employers actually use.</p>
<h1>Start by defining what you want to be found for</h1>
<p>Before rewriting your profile, decide what type of opportunity you want.</p>
<p>A profile trying to attract every technical role usually becomes vague.</p>
<p>Write down:</p>
<h2>Target role</h2>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>Senior Backend Engineer</li>
<li>Frontend Product Engineer</li>
<li>Full-Stack TypeScript Developer</li>
<li>Mobile iOS Engineer</li>
<li>Data Engineer</li>
<li>Platform Engineer</li>
<li>Engineering Manager</li>
<li>Application Security Engineer</li>
<li>Junior Software Developer</li>
</ul>
<h2>Primary technologies</h2>
<p>Choose the technologies you use confidently and want to continue using.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>TypeScript</li>
<li>React</li>
<li>Next.js</li>
<li>Node.js</li>
<li>PostgreSQL</li>
<li>Python</li>
<li>Go</li>
<li>Rust</li>
<li>Java</li>
<li>Kotlin</li>
<li>Swift</li>
<li>AWS</li>
<li>Kubernetes</li>
</ul>
<h2>Preferred problems</h2>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>API design;</li>
<li>performance;</li>
<li>developer tooling;</li>
<li>data pipelines;</li>
<li>payments;</li>
<li>security;</li>
<li>accessibility;</li>
<li>platform reliability;</li>
<li>mobile applications;</li>
<li>distributed systems;</li>
<li>product engineering;</li>
<li>internal tools.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Work preferences</h2>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>remote within the United States;</li>
<li>remote across North America;</li>
<li>hybrid in Chicago;</li>
<li>contract work;</li>
<li>full-time only;</li>
<li>individual-contributor roles;</li>
<li>management;</li>
<li>startup teams;</li>
<li>enterprise environments;</li>
<li>open-source companies.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Exclusions</h2>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>no relocation;</li>
<li>no agency contracting;</li>
<li>no management roles;</li>
<li>no unpaid trial work;</li>
<li>no on-call-heavy positions;</li>
<li>no roles requiring frequent travel.</li>
</ul>
<p>This definition becomes the foundation for the rest of the profile.</p>
<h1>Use a recognizable professional title</h1>
<p>Your internal title may not match the language employers search for.</p>
<p>A company might call you:</p>
<ul>
<li>Software Craftsperson III</li>
<li>Technical Member of Staff</li>
<li>Product Builder</li>
<li>Application Specialist</li>
<li>Solutions Developer</li>
<li>Digital Engineer</li>
<li>Technology Consultant</li>
</ul>
<p>Those titles may be meaningful internally but weak in search.</p>
<p>Keep the accurate employer title in your experience section, but use a recognizable market title in your headline or summary when appropriate.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Senior Backend Engineer specializing in Go, PostgreSQL, and distributed services</p>
</blockquote>
<p>or:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Staff Platform Engineer focused on Kubernetes, developer infrastructure, and reliability</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Do not inflate your level.</p>
<p>Translate unusual terminology without misrepresenting your responsibility.</p>
<h1>Write a headline that earns the click</h1>
<p>The headline is one of the highest-value sections of your profile.</p>
<p>It may appear:</p>
<ul>
<li>in search results;</li>
<li>beside your name;</li>
<li>in recommendations;</li>
<li>in recruiter dashboards;</li>
<li>in messages;</li>
<li>in profile previews.</li>
</ul>
<p>A weak headline wastes this space.</p>
<h2>Weak headline</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Software Engineer at Acme Corporation</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This tells the reader your employer and a broad category.</p>
<p>It does not explain:</p>
<ul>
<li>frontend or backend;</li>
<li>technology;</li>
<li>seniority;</li>
<li>specialization;</li>
<li>availability;</li>
<li>preferred work.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Stronger headline structure</h2>
<p>Use:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>[Recognizable role] · [Core technologies or specialty] · [Optional preference or outcome]</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Examples:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Senior Backend Engineer · Go, PostgreSQL, distributed systems · Open to remote roles</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Frontend Product Engineer · React, TypeScript, accessibility, design systems</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Data Engineer · Python, SQL, Airflow, Snowflake · Batch and streaming pipelines</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>iOS Engineer · Swift, SwiftUI, mobile performance · Remote US</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Junior Full-Stack Developer · React, Node.js, PostgreSQL · Open to entry-level roles</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Engineering Manager · Developer platforms, team growth, remote leadership</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Your headline should describe what you want to be hired to do next, provided it remains truthful to your experience.</p>
<h1>Avoid headline keyword overload</h1>
<p>A headline such as this is difficult to trust:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Senior Staff Full-Stack Cloud DevOps AI Blockchain React Node Python Java AWS Azure GCP Engineer</p>
</blockquote>
<p>It signals breadth without focus.</p>
<p>Choose the strongest identity.</p>
<p>You can include additional skills elsewhere.</p>
<h1>Write a summary that answers three questions</h1>
<p>Your summary should help the reader understand:</p>
<ol>
<li>What do you do?</li>
<li>What evidence supports that?</li>
<li>What do you want next?</li>
</ol>
<p>Three to five short paragraphs are usually enough.</p>
<h1>Paragraph one: explain what you do</h1>
<p>Be concrete.</p>
<p>Weak:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Passionate software engineer with a proven track record of delivering innovative solutions in fast-paced environments.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I am a backend engineer focused on TypeScript, PostgreSQL, and API design. I usually work on transaction-heavy systems where correctness, observability, and reliable failure handling matter.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Another example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I build accessible frontend products with React and TypeScript. My work usually sits at the intersection of design systems, complex application state, performance, and customer-facing workflows.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This immediately gives the reader a technical frame.</p>
<h1>Paragraph two: provide evidence</h1>
<p>Include one or two outcomes.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>At Acme, I redesigned the subscription-entitlement service, reduced duplicate billing events through idempotency controls, and improved p95 API latency from approximately 700 ms to 180 ms.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Or:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I led the migration of a legacy dashboard into a shared React design system, reduced duplicated component code, and improved keyboard and screen-reader behavior across the primary account workflow.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Evidence converts claims into credibility.</p>
<h1>Paragraph three: explain what you want</h1>
<p>This helps recruiters decide whether contacting you makes sense.</p>
<p>Example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I am interested in senior backend roles at product-focused teams where I can own services from design through production. I am open to remote positions within the United States and am not currently pursuing engineering-management roles.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Or:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I am looking for a junior frontend or full-stack role with regular code review and mentorship. I am especially interested in developer tools, education, and accessibility-focused products.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This filters irrelevant messages without preventing unexpected opportunities.</p>
<h1>Write in first person</h1>
<p>First person usually sounds more direct and human.</p>
<p>Prefer:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>I build backend services for data-heavy products.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Alex is a results-oriented technology professional with a passion for excellence.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Third-person summaries often sound like generic corporate biographies.</p>
<h1>Remove empty adjectives</h1>
<p>Words such as these add little without evidence:</p>
<ul>
<li>passionate;</li>
<li>innovative;</li>
<li>dynamic;</li>
<li>results-driven;</li>
<li>highly motivated;</li>
<li>visionary;</li>
<li>world-class;</li>
<li>strategic;</li>
<li>detail-oriented;</li>
<li>collaborative;</li>
<li>hard-working.</li>
</ul>
<p>You may genuinely possess these qualities.</p>
<p>Demonstrate them through work.</p>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Highly collaborative engineer</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Coordinated an API migration across product, mobile, support, and platform teams while maintaining backward compatibility for older clients.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Name technologies precisely</h1>
<p>Search systems and human reviewers recognize specific terms.</p>
<p>Prefer:</p>
<ul>
<li>TypeScript</li>
<li>React</li>
<li>Next.js</li>
<li>PostgreSQL</li>
<li>Kubernetes</li>
<li>Amazon Web Services</li>
<li>AWS</li>
<li>GraphQL</li>
<li>REST APIs</li>
<li>OpenTelemetry</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid replacing specific technology names with vague categories such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>modern web tools;</li>
<li>cloud technologies;</li>
<li>database systems;</li>
<li>frontend frameworks;</li>
<li>backend development.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use both the full name and common abbreviation when helpful.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Amazon Web Services (AWS)</p>
</blockquote>
<p>or:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD)</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Do not repeat every term unnaturally.</p>
<p>Place them where they belong.</p>
<h1>Build a focused skills section</h1>
<p>Your skills section should represent the work you can discuss and want to perform.</p>
<p>A useful grouping may include:</p>
<h2>Languages</h2>
<ul>
<li>TypeScript</li>
<li>JavaScript</li>
<li>Python</li>
<li>SQL</li>
</ul>
<h2>Frontend</h2>
<ul>
<li>React</li>
<li>Next.js</li>
<li>HTML</li>
<li>CSS</li>
<li>Tailwind CSS</li>
<li>Accessibility</li>
</ul>
<h2>Backend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Node.js</li>
<li>REST APIs</li>
<li>GraphQL</li>
<li>Authentication</li>
<li>Background jobs</li>
</ul>
<h2>Data</h2>
<ul>
<li>PostgreSQL</li>
<li>Redis</li>
<li>Data modeling</li>
<li>Query optimization</li>
</ul>
<h2>Infrastructure and tools</h2>
<ul>
<li>AWS</li>
<li>Docker</li>
<li>Kubernetes</li>
<li>GitHub Actions</li>
<li>OpenTelemetry</li>
<li>Git</li>
</ul>
<p>This is clearer than a single list of fifty unrelated terms.</p>
<h1>Order skills by the work you want</h1>
<p>The first skills a recruiter sees should reflect your target role.</p>
<p>A developer applying for backend roles should not lead with Photoshop because they used it several years ago.</p>
<p>A frontend engineer interested in accessibility should not bury it below a long list of minor packages.</p>
<p>Order skills by:</p>
<ol>
<li>relevance;</li>
<li>strength;</li>
<li>recency;</li>
<li>target-role importance.</li>
</ol>
<h1>Remove skills you cannot defend</h1>
<p>Do not list a technology merely because:</p>
<ul>
<li>you completed one tutorial;</li>
<li>you watched a course;</li>
<li>another teammate used it;</li>
<li>it appears in job descriptions;</li>
<li>an AI tool added it to your profile.</li>
</ul>
<p>Assume an interviewer may ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>Where did you use it?</li>
<li>What did you build?</li>
<li>What problems did you encounter?</li>
<li>What would you do differently?</li>
<li>How strong are you with it today?</li>
</ul>
<p>Honest skill lists build trust.</p>
<h1>Turn experience into evidence</h1>
<p>A profile should not simply reproduce the responsibilities from your job description.</p>
<p>Weak experience bullet:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Responsible for developing backend services.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Built and operated Node.js services supporting account provisioning for more than 40,000 customer organizations.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Weak:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Worked with PostgreSQL databases.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Reduced a reporting query from 18 seconds to under two seconds by redesigning indexes and removing repeated joins across historical records.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Weak:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Participated in code reviews.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Reviewed backend and database changes across a six-engineer team and introduced a pull-request checklist covering migrations, observability, failure behavior, and rollback.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Use an impact-oriented bullet structure</h1>
<p>A useful pattern is:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>[Action] + [system or problem] + [technical approach] + [result or significance]</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Examples:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Reduced image-processing costs by 31% by moving duplicate transformations into a content-addressed cache.</p>
</blockquote>

</blockquote>

</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Built an offline synchronization workflow for field technicians using local persistence, retry queues, and conflict detection.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Introduced automated accessibility checks and manual keyboard testing across the checkout flow, resolving 23 high-priority issues before launch.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Use numbers carefully</h1>
<p>Metrics make work more concrete when they are truthful and meaningful.</p>
<p>Useful numbers may include:</p>
<ul>

<li>cost;</li>
<li>users;</li>
<li>transactions;</li>

<li>failure rate;</li>
<li>test time;</li>
<li>build time;</li>
<li>support volume;</li>
<li>adoption;</li>
<li>revenue impact;</li>
<li>hours saved;</li>
<li>team size;</li>
<li>storage volume.</li>
</ul>
<p>Examples:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Cut p95 response time from 1.2 seconds to 280 milliseconds.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Reduced monthly infrastructure spend by approximately $4,000.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Supported more than 3 million API requests per day.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p>Automated a manual reconciliation workflow that previously required eight staff hours per week.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>Use language such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>approximately;</li>
<li>about;</li>
<li>more than;</li>
<li>fewer than;</li>
<li>roughly;</li>
</ul>
<p>when exact figures are unavailable.</p>
<h1>Explain scale without exaggerating</h1>

<ul>

<li>long-lived systems;</li>
<li>accessibility requirements;</li>
<li>offline use;</li>
<li>operational constraints.</li>
</ul>

<blockquote>

</blockquote>
<p>That is meaningful even without internet-scale traffic.</p>
<h1>Clarify your individual contribution</h1>

</blockquote>

</ul>
<p>Record enough context to write truthful profile bullets later.</p>
<p>Do not store confidential company information in an insecure personal system.</p>
<p>A simple entry might include:</p>
<ul>
<li>problem;</li>
<li>your role;</li>
<li>action;</li>
<li>technology;</li>
<li>result;</li>
<li>approved public wording.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Shape recruiter outreach through specificity</h1>
<p>A vague profile may attract more messages but lower relevance.</p>
<p>A precise profile can produce fewer, better conversations.</p>
<p>Consider stating:</p>
<ul>
<li>target role;</li>
<li>preferred technologies;</li>
<li>employment type;</li>
<li>work location;</li>
<li>level;</li>
<li>industries of interest;</li>
<li>excluded arrangements.</li>
</ul>
<p>Example:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Interested in senior backend or platform roles using Go, TypeScript, or Rust. Remote US only. Not pursuing management, relocation, or short-term agency contracts.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Some recruiters will ignore preferences.</p>
<p>Many will not.</p>
<p>The profile still improves the filtering process.</p>
<h1>Decide how public you want the search to be</h1>
<p>Depending on your situation, you may choose:</p>
<ul>
<li>publicly open to work;</li>
<li>visible only to recruiters;</li>
<li>no explicit status;</li>
<li>targeted outreach only.</li>
</ul>
<p>Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>current employer policies;</li>
<li>professional relationships;</li>
<li>privacy;</li>
<li>urgency;</li>
<li>desired message volume.</li>
</ul>
<p>You can still improve searchability without announcing an active job search publicly.</p>
<h1>Use activity strategically</h1>
<p>You do not need to post daily.</p>
<p>Useful activity may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>sharing a technical lesson;</li>
<li>commenting thoughtfully on engineering discussions;</li>
<li>publishing a project update;</li>
<li>writing a short case study;</li>
<li>congratulating teammates;</li>
<li>participating in open source;</li>
<li>answering a useful technical question.</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid turning your profile into a stream of generic motivational content unrelated to your work.</p>
<p>Consistency and relevance matter more than frequency.</p>
<h1>Review privacy and security settings</h1>
<p>Check:</p>
<ul>
<li>who can see your email;</li>
<li>who can message you;</li>
<li>whether your employer sees activity;</li>
<li>whether contact details are public;</li>
<li>whether uploaded documents expose personal information;</li>
<li>whether portfolio projects contain secrets;</li>
<li>whether location details are more precise than necessary.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not publish:</p>
<ul>
<li>full home address;</li>
<li>date of birth;</li>
<li>government identification;</li>
<li>bank information;</li>
<li>private phone number unless intentional;</li>
<li>confidential employment documents.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Watch for recruiter and job scams</h1>
<p>A discoverable profile may attract fraudulent contact.</p>
<p>Warning signs include:</p>
<ul>
<li>offers without interviews;</li>
<li>unusually high compensation for vague work;</li>
<li>communication only through encrypted chat;</li>
<li>personal email domains;</li>
<li>requests to pay for equipment;</li>
<li>checks sent for purchases;</li>
<li>requests for banking details before a legitimate offer;</li>
<li>requests for identity documents unusually early;</li>
<li>cloned company websites;</li>
<li>recruiters who cannot be verified.</li>
</ul>
<p>Verify:</p>
<ul>
<li>company domain;</li>
<li>recruiter identity;</li>
<li>careers page;</li>
<li>official job listing;</li>
<li>public company presence.</li>
</ul>
<p>Never pay to receive a job.</p>
<h1>Use AI tools carefully when improving your profile</h1>
<p>AI can help you:</p>
<ul>
<li>shorten text;</li>
<li>organize sections;</li>
<li>identify vague phrases;</li>
<li>generate alternative headlines;</li>
<li>improve grammar;</li>
<li>turn notes into draft bullets;</li>
<li>compare your profile with job descriptions.</li>
</ul>
<p>You remain responsible for every claim.</p>
<p>Do not allow an AI tool to:</p>
<ul>
<li>invent metrics;</li>
<li>add technologies you have not used;</li>
<li>inflate your level;</li>
<li>fabricate leadership;</li>
<li>expose confidential information;</li>
<li>transform team work into individual ownership.</li>
</ul>
<p>A useful process is:</p>
<ol>
<li>Write factual notes.</li>
<li>Ask AI to organize them.</li>
<li>Verify every sentence.</li>
<li>restore your natural voice;</li>
<li>Remove exaggeration.</li>
<li>Confirm confidentiality.</li>
</ol>
<h1>Ask another person to perform the ten-second test</h1>
<p>Give your profile to:</p>
<ul>
<li>a developer;</li>
<li>hiring manager;</li>
<li>recruiter;</li>
<li>mentor;</li>
<li>trusted peer.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask them to look for ten seconds and then answer:</p>
<ul>
<li>What kind of developer am I?</li>
<li>What level do I appear to be?</li>
<li>Which technologies do I use?</li>
<li>What problems do I solve?</li>
<li>What role do I want?</li>
<li>Where can I work?</li>
<li>What evidence stood out?</li>
</ul>
<p>Their answers reveal what your profile actually communicates.</p>
<h1>Audit searchability</h1>
<p>Search your profile text for the terms associated with your target role.</p>
<p>For example, a senior backend engineer might expect to find:</p>
<ul>
<li>Senior Backend Engineer</li>
<li>API design</li>
<li>PostgreSQL</li>
<li>distributed systems</li>
<li>observability</li>
<li>AWS</li>
<li>testing</li>
<li>production</li>
<li>incident response</li>
<li>mentoring</li>
</ul>
<p>A mobile developer might expect:</p>
<ul>
<li>iOS or Android</li>
<li>Swift or Kotlin</li>
<li>SwiftUI or Jetpack Compose</li>
<li>App Store or Google Play</li>
<li>mobile performance</li>
<li>offline behavior</li>
<li>crash reporting</li>
<li>accessibility</li>
<li>release management</li>
</ul>
<p>Add only terms that truthfully describe your work.</p>
<h1>A complete profile structure</h1>
<p>A useful developer profile may follow this order:</p>
<h2>Name</h2>
<p>Use the professional name you consistently use across applications.</p>
<h2>Headline</h2>
<p>State role, specialty, technologies, and relevant preference.</p>
<h2>Location and work preference</h2>
<p>Include city or region, time zone, remote status, and relocation preference.</p>
<h2>Summary</h2>
<p>Explain:</p>
<ul>
<li>what you do;</li>
<li>evidence;</li>
<li>what you want.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Core skills</h2>
<p>Group and order relevant skills.</p>
<h2>Experience</h2>
<p>Use impact-focused bullets.</p>
<h2>Featured projects</h2>
<p>Show proof of work.</p>
<h2>Education and training</h2>
<p>Include relevant degrees, courses, apprenticeships, or certifications.</p>
<h2>Open source and community work</h2>
<p>Include meaningful contributions.</p>
<h2>Writing and talks</h2>
<p>Add technical communication evidence.</p>
<h2>Links and contact</h2>
<p>Make the next step easy.</p>
<h1>Sample backend developer profile</h1>
<h2>Headline</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Senior Backend Engineer · TypeScript, PostgreSQL, distributed services · Remote US</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Summary</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>I build backend systems for subscription and marketplace products using TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL, and AWS. My strongest work is in API design, data integrity, background processing, observability, and production debugging.</p>
<p>At Acme, I redesigned the entitlement service used by more than 30,000 customer accounts, reduced duplicate events through idempotency controls, and cut p95 response time from approximately 900 ms to 240 ms.</p>
<p>I am interested in senior individual-contributor roles where I can own services from design through production. I am open to remote US positions and am not currently pursuing engineering management.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Skills</h2>
<ul>
<li>TypeScript</li>
<li>Node.js</li>
<li>PostgreSQL</li>
<li>REST APIs</li>
<li>GraphQL</li>
<li>Redis</li>
<li>AWS</li>
<li>Docker</li>
<li>OpenTelemetry</li>
<li>GitHub Actions</li>
</ul>
<h1>Sample frontend developer profile</h1>
<h2>Headline</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Frontend Product Engineer · React, TypeScript, accessibility, design systems</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Summary</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>I build accessible web applications with React and TypeScript, with a focus on complex product workflows, design systems, performance, and reliable state management.</p>
<p>I recently led the migration of a customer dashboard into a shared component system, reduced duplicated interface code, and improved keyboard and screen-reader support across the primary account flows.</p>
<p>I am interested in senior frontend or product-engineering roles at teams that treat accessibility and design quality as engineering responsibilities.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Sample junior developer profile</h1>
<h2>Headline</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Junior Full-Stack Developer · React, TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL · Open to entry-level roles</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Summary</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>I am a junior full-stack developer building and deploying TypeScript applications with React, Node.js, and PostgreSQL.</p>
<p>My primary project is a multi-organization scheduling platform with account authentication, role-based permissions, automated tests, and a public deployment. I redesigned the membership model after early users needed access to several organizations.</p>
<p>Before moving into software development, I worked in operations, where I gained experience documenting workflows, investigating errors, and communicating with nontechnical users. I am looking for a junior role with code review, mentorship, and meaningful production work.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Profile checklist</h1>
<p>Before publishing or updating your profile, confirm that:</p>
<ul>
<li>your headline identifies a recognizable role;</li>
<li>your primary technologies are named;</li>
<li>your target seniority is accurate;</li>
<li>your location and work preferences are clear;</li>
<li>the first summary paragraph explains what you do;</li>
<li>the summary contains evidence;</li>
<li>the summary states what you want next;</li>
<li>experience bullets describe outcomes;</li>
<li>metrics are truthful;</li>
<li>skills are ordered by relevance;</li>
<li>weak or obsolete skills are removed;</li>
<li>projects include working links;</li>
<li>project descriptions explain your contribution;</li>
<li>GitHub repositories are curated;</li>
<li>dates match your résumé;</li>
<li>your photo is current where used;</li>
<li>contact information works;</li>
<li>private information is protected;</li>
<li>no confidential company information is exposed;</li>
<li>spelling and formatting are consistent;</li>
<li>your availability status is current.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Ten-second skim checklist</h1>
<p>At the top of the profile, a reader should be able to identify:</p>
<ul>
<li>role;</li>
<li>level;</li>
<li>specialty;</li>
<li>primary technologies;</li>
<li>location;</li>
<li>remote preference;</li>
<li>one credibility signal;</li>
<li>availability.</li>
</ul>
<p>If one of these is missing, decide whether it should be added.</p>
<h1>Quarterly maintenance checklist</h1>
<p>Every three months:</p>
<ul>
<li>update your current role;</li>
<li>add one meaningful achievement;</li>
<li>review skills;</li>
<li>remove outdated preferences;</li>
<li>test public links;</li>
<li>update projects;</li>
<li>check message settings;</li>
<li>review the summary;</li>
<li>verify availability;</li>
<li>review privacy;</li>
<li>compare the profile with current target roles.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Common profile mistakes</h1>
<h2>Using only an employer title</h2>
<p>The headline provides no searchable specialization.</p>
<h2>Writing a summary full of adjectives</h2>
<p>The reader sees claims without evidence.</p>
<h2>Listing every technology ever touched</h2>
<p>The profile loses focus and credibility.</p>
<h2>Describing responsibilities instead of outcomes</h2>
<p>The experience section looks interchangeable with every other candidate.</p>
<h2>Hiding work preferences</h2>
<p>Recruiters contact you for roles you cannot accept.</p>
<h2>Leaving links broken</h2>
<p>Proof of work becomes a negative signal.</p>
<h2>Using inconsistent dates</h2>
<p>The profile creates unnecessary doubt.</p>
<h2>Claiming team work as personal ownership</h2>
<p>Trust declines during the interview.</p>
<h2>Waiting until an emergency to update</h2>
<p>Important achievements are forgotten.</p>
<h2>Posting constantly without substance</h2>
<p>Activity creates noise rather than credibility.</p>
<h2>Copying AI-generated language without review</h2>
<p>The profile sounds generic or contains invented claims.</p>
<h1>Phrases to remove</h1>
<p>Replace phrases such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>passionate developer;</li>
<li>coding ninja;</li>
<li>results-driven professional;</li>
<li>team player;</li>
<li>strategic thinker;</li>
<li>hard worker;</li>
<li>self-starter;</li>
<li>dynamic environment;</li>
<li>innovative solutions;</li>
<li>excellent communicator;</li>
<li>extensive experience;</li>
<li>proven track record;</li>
<li>cutting-edge technologies.</li>
</ul>
<p>Replace them with evidence.</p>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Excellent communicator</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Wrote implementation proposals and asynchronous handoffs for a team working across four time zones.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Results-driven engineer</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Reduced deployment failures by 38% after introducing pre-release validation and rollback checks.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>The takeaway</h1>
<p>A developer profile has two primary jobs:</p>
<ol>
<li>Appear in relevant searches.</li>
<li>Give the reader enough confidence to contact you.</li>
</ol>
<p>To accomplish both:</p>
<ul>
<li>choose a clear target identity;</li>
<li>use a recognizable professional title;</li>
<li>name technologies accurately;</li>
<li>write a headline that communicates role and specialty;</li>
<li>keep the summary short, specific, and human;</li>
<li>include one or two concrete outcomes;</li>
<li>state what you want next;</li>
<li>turn responsibilities into evidence;</li>
<li>order skills according to your target role;</li>
<li>show projects and real work;</li>
<li>make location and employment preferences clear;</li>
<li>keep dates and claims accurate;</li>
<li>protect confidential information;</li>
<li>maintain the profile before the next urgent job search.</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal is not maximum visibility.</p>
<p>It is <strong>relevant visibility</strong>.</p>
<p>You do not need every recruiter to find you.</p>
<p>You need the right hiring teams to understand what you can do, what you want to do next, and why beginning a conversation with you is worth their time.</p>
