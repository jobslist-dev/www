e:Tec6f,<p>Backend developer job descriptions often begin with a list of programming languages and end with a vague promise that the candidate will build “scalable, high-performance systems.”</p>
<p>That tells experienced backend engineers very little.</p>
<p>A strong candidate wants to understand the actual shape of the work:</p>
<ul>
<li>What does the system do?</li>
<li>Who depends on it?</li>
<li>How much traffic or data does it handle?</li>
<li>Is the architecture a monolith, modular application, service platform, or event-driven system?</li>
<li>Is the main challenge reliability, integrations, data modeling, performance, security, or migration?</li>
<li>Which systems would this person own?</li>
<li>Who owns infrastructure?</li>
<li>How often does production fail?</li>
<li>Is there an on-call rotation?</li>
<li>How much technical debt exists?</li>
<li>What authority would the engineer have to improve it?</li>
</ul>
<p>“Backend developer” can describe several very different jobs.</p>
<p>One role may involve designing APIs and relational schemas for a growing product. Another may focus on high-throughput distributed systems. Another may involve integrating payment providers, modernizing a legacy application, operating data pipelines, or maintaining a small but business-critical internal platform.</p>
<p>All of those can be valuable engineering roles.</p>
<p>They attract different candidates.</p>
<p>The purpose of a good job description is not to make every backend engineer want the job. It is to help the right engineers recognize that the problems, expectations, and working environment match what they want to do next.</p>
<p>This guide includes a complete copy-and-paste backend developer job description template, followed by practical advice for defining the role, screening applications, designing interviews, evaluating operational judgment, and avoiding the most common backend-hiring mistakes.</p>
<h1>Backend Developer Job Description Template</h1>
<p>Replace each bracketed section with accurate information about your company and role.</p>
<p>Remove anything that does not apply.</p>
<p>Do not publish placeholder language or responsibilities copied from another company.</p>
<h2>[Level] Backend Developer at [Company]</h2>
<p><strong>Location:</strong> [Remote within approved region / Hybrid in city / On-site in city]</p>
<p><strong>Employment type:</strong> [Full-time / Part-time / Contract / Contract-to-hire]</p>
<p><strong>Compensation:</strong> [Currency and salary range], plus [bonus, equity, or other compensation]</p>
<p><strong>Level:</strong> [Junior / Mid-level / Senior / Staff]</p>
<p><strong>Reports to:</strong> [Engineering Manager / Director of Engineering / CTO / Technical Lead]</p>
<p><strong>Team:</strong> [Team name or product domain]</p>
<h2>About [Company]</h2>
<p>[Company] builds [plain-language description of the product or platform] for [customers or users].</p>
<p>Our backend systems currently support:</p>
<ul>
<li>[number] customers or organizations;</li>
<li>[number] active users;</li>
<li>approximately [number] requests, transactions, jobs, or events per day;</li>
<li>approximately [data volume];</li>
<li>[availability, latency, or regulatory requirement, when relevant].</li>
</ul>
<p>We are hiring a [level] Backend Developer to own [specific service, workflow, platform area, or business capability].</p>
<p>The primary challenge during the first [six or twelve] months will be [specific technical or product outcome].</p>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Acme builds inventory and order-management software for regional distributors. Our platform supports approximately 3,000 customer organizations, processes 12 million inventory events per day, and integrates with more than 40 warehouse and accounting systems.</p>
<p>We are hiring a Senior Backend Developer to own the order-allocation and inventory-reservation services. The first six months will focus on eliminating duplicate reservations, improving observability, and moving several high-risk workflows out of a tightly coupled legacy module.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This tells candidates far more than:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Join our fast-growing team and help us build scalable APIs.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Why this role exists</h1>
<p>Explain the business or technical reason for hiring.</p>
<p>Possible reasons include:</p>
<ul>
<li>the customer base is growing;</li>
<li>an existing engineer is moving to another team;</li>
<li>a legacy system needs modernization;</li>
<li>the company is creating a new service;</li>
<li>reliability has become a business risk;</li>
<li>integrations are becoming difficult to maintain;</li>
<li>the team needs stronger database expertise;</li>
<li>the platform is moving from batch to event-driven processing;</li>
<li>the organization needs broader technical ownership;</li>
<li>an internal capability is being brought in-house.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Order volume has doubled during the past year, and several workflows that were originally designed for a smaller customer base now create locking, retry, and reconciliation problems. This role exists to improve the system without interrupting current customers.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Candidates deserve to know whether they are joining to create, stabilize, migrate, scale, or repair.</p>
<h1>What you will own</h1>
<p>This role will own:</p>
<ul>
<li>[service, API, workflow, platform capability, or domain];</li>
<li>[relevant database schemas and migrations];</li>
<li>[production reliability and observability];</li>
<li>[technical documentation and operational runbooks];</li>
<li>[technical relationships with frontend, mobile, data, platform, or external partners].</li>
</ul>
<p>Shared ownership includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>[architecture with senior or staff engineers];</li>
<li>[roadmap decisions with product];</li>
<li>[cloud infrastructure with the platform team];</li>
<li>[security controls with application security];</li>
<li>[incident response with site reliability engineering].</li>
</ul>
<p>Owned elsewhere:</p>
<ul>
<li>[frontend implementation];</li>
<li>[infrastructure provisioning];</li>
<li>[data science];</li>
<li>[customer support];</li>
<li>[product management];</li>
<li>[people management].</li>
</ul>
<p>Defining boundaries prevents candidates from discovering after joining that “backend ownership” also means independently managing infrastructure, support, QA, security, and product requirements.</p>
<h1>What you will do</h1>
<p>You will:</p>
<ul>
<li>Design, build, test, deploy, and operate backend services using [language and framework].</li>
<li>Own [specific system or domain] from technical design through production support.</li>
<li>Translate product requirements into clear APIs, data models, background processes, and failure-handling behavior.</li>
<li>Design and evolve relational or non-relational schemas without unnecessarily disrupting existing consumers.</li>
<li>Build APIs used by [web applications, mobile clients, partners, internal teams, or external developers].</li>
<li>Improve system reliability, observability, security, latency, and cost efficiency.</li>
<li>Investigate production incidents and convert lessons into stronger tests, alerts, safeguards, and operational documentation.</li>
<li>Write migrations, backfills, and compatibility plans for data and API changes.</li>
<li>Review pull requests and provide constructive technical feedback.</li>
<li>Collaborate with frontend, mobile, data, security, platform, product, and customer-facing teams.</li>
<li>Document technical decisions, system behavior, ownership, and operational procedures.</li>
<li>Participate in [on-call rotation], described in detail below.</li>
<li>Help improve team development practices, release safety, and engineering standards.</li>
<li>Mentor [junior or mid-level engineers], when appropriate to the role.</li>
<li>Evaluate technical trade-offs and avoid unnecessary complexity.</li>
</ul>
<p>Customize this list around the actual work.</p>
<p>If the engineer will spend most of their time on integrations, migrations, database work, or platform reliability, say so directly.</p>
<h1>What success looks like</h1>
<h2>During the first 30 days</h2>
<p>You will:</p>
<ul>
<li>complete technical and organizational onboarding;</li>
<li>configure the local development environment;</li>
<li>understand the product, users, and critical workflows;</li>
<li>learn the architecture and deployment process;</li>
<li>review current service-level indicators and recurring incidents;</li>
<li>ship a small, low-risk production change;</li>
<li>identify missing or outdated documentation.</li>
</ul>
<h2>During the first 60 days</h2>
<p>You will:</p>
<ul>
<li>own a contained backend feature or reliability improvement;</li>
<li>independently investigate routine defects;</li>
<li>contribute to API and schema design;</li>
<li>participate in production monitoring and incident review;</li>
<li>improve at least one test, dashboard, alert, or operational runbook;</li>
<li>build relationships with the teams consuming your services.</li>
</ul>
<h2>During the first 90 days</h2>
<p>You will:</p>
<ul>
<li>deliver a meaningful backend project end to end;</li>
<li>manage a schema or API change safely;</li>
<li>communicate technical risk and trade-offs clearly;</li>
<li>participate effectively in on-call or production support;</li>
<li>contribute to technical planning;</li>
<li>establish goals for broader system ownership.</li>
</ul>
<h2>During the first year</h2>
<p>You may:</p>
<ul>
<li>own [service or technical domain];</li>
<li>improve [reliability, latency, cost, or delivery metric];</li>
<li>lead [migration, modernization, integration, or scaling initiative];</li>
<li>mentor other developers;</li>
<li>influence architecture across related systems;</li>
<li>improve the team&#39;s incident, testing, or release practices.</li>
</ul>
<p>Success measures should reflect outcomes, not merely ticket volume.</p>
<h1>What we are looking for</h1>
<p>You may be a strong fit if you have:</p>
<ul>
<li>[number]+ years building backend or server-side software, or equivalent evidence of production capability.</li>
<li>Strong experience with [one essential language or ecosystem].</li>
<li>Experience designing and maintaining APIs.</li>
<li>Practical database experience, including schema design, indexing, query analysis, migrations, and data integrity.</li>
<li>Experience testing backend behavior at the unit, integration, or contract level.</li>
<li>Experience deploying and operating software after release.</li>
<li>Strong debugging skills across application, database, queue, cache, network, or infrastructure boundaries.</li>
<li>Ability to break ambiguous problems into manageable technical work.</li>
<li>Clear written and verbal communication.</li>
<li>Understanding of authentication, authorization, input validation, secrets, and secure service design.</li>
<li>Ability to reason about failure, retries, idempotency, timeouts, and partial completion.</li>
<li>Experience collaborating with engineers and nonengineering partners.</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep the required section focused.</p>
<p>Do not list every technology in your environment as mandatory.</p>
<h1>Nice to have</h1>
<p>Experience with any of the following may be helpful:</p>
<ul>
<li>[message queue or event-streaming platform];</li>
<li>[cache or distributed data store];</li>
<li>[cloud provider];</li>
<li>infrastructure as code;</li>
<li>containers or orchestration;</li>
<li>service observability;</li>
<li>high-availability systems;</li>
<li>large data migrations;</li>
<li>multi-tenant architecture;</li>
<li>developer-facing APIs;</li>
<li>regulated environments;</li>
<li>domain knowledge in [fintech, healthcare, logistics, education, security, or another field];</li>
<li>open-source backend development;</li>
<li>technical mentoring.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you would reject a candidate for lacking an item, it is not genuinely optional.</p>
<h1>What you do not need</h1>
<p>You do not need:</p>
<ul>
<li>professional experience with every tool in our stack;</li>
<li>a computer-science degree;</li>
<li>previous employment at a famous technology company;</li>
<li>experience at internet-scale traffic;</li>
<li>expertise in both frontend and infrastructure;</li>
<li>a public GitHub profile;</li>
<li>to meet every optional qualification.</li>
</ul>
<p>Include only promises your hiring process will honor.</p>
<h1>Our backend stack</h1>
<p><strong>Primary language:</strong> [Go / Java / Kotlin / C# / TypeScript / Python / Ruby / PHP / Rust]</p>
<p><strong>Framework:</strong> [Spring Boot / ASP.NET Core / NestJS / FastAPI / Django / Rails / Laravel / Axum / another framework]</p>
<p><strong>Primary database:</strong> [PostgreSQL / MySQL / SQL Server / MongoDB / another system]</p>
<p><strong>Caching:</strong> [Redis / Memcached / none]</p>
<p><strong>Messaging:</strong> [Kafka / RabbitMQ / SQS / Pub/Sub / NATS / none]</p>
<p><strong>APIs:</strong> [REST / GraphQL / gRPC / webhooks / event contracts]</p>
<p><strong>Cloud:</strong> [AWS / Azure / Google Cloud / managed platform]</p>
<p><strong>Infrastructure:</strong> [Kubernetes / serverless / virtual machines / managed containers]</p>
<p><strong>CI/CD:</strong> [GitHub Actions / GitLab CI / Buildkite / CircleCI / another system]</p>
<p><strong>Observability:</strong> [OpenTelemetry / Datadog / Grafana / Prometheus / Sentry / New Relic]</p>
<p><strong>Testing:</strong> [unit, integration, contract, end-to-end, performance testing]</p>
<p><strong>Architecture:</strong> [modular monolith / service-oriented / event-driven / mixed architecture]</p>
<p><strong>Deployment cadence:</strong> [several times per day / weekly / scheduled releases]</p>
<p><strong>Current technical reality:</strong> [one honest sentence about the codebase].</p>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The newer order-processing services are well tested and independently deployable. Several supplier integrations still run through a legacy import module with limited observability, and replacing that module will be an important part of this role.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>That sentence creates credibility.</p>
<p>Experienced engineers assume technical debt exists. They want to know what kind of debt they are being hired to address.</p>
<h1>Describe the system honestly</h1>
<p>Backend candidates often care more about the shape of the system than the framework.</p>
<p>Explain whether the architecture is:</p>
<ul>
<li>a monolith;</li>
<li>a modular monolith;</li>
<li>several services;</li>
<li>event driven;</li>
<li>heavily integrated with third parties;</li>
<li>primarily batch processing;</li>
<li>latency sensitive;</li>
<li>correctness sensitive;</li>
<li>data intensive;</li>
<li>regulated;</li>
<li>internal rather than customer facing.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The product is primarily a modular monolith with three supporting services for notifications, file processing, and search. We are not planning to split every domain into microservices. New boundaries are introduced only when operational or ownership needs justify them.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>That attracts candidates who appreciate deliberate architecture rather than trend-driven decomposition.</p>
<h1>Publish real scale</h1>
<p>Avoid adjectives such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>massive;</li>
<li>enormous;</li>
<li>hyperscale;</li>
<li>high-volume;</li>
<li>enterprise-scale.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use numbers or meaningful operational context.</p>
<p>Useful indicators include:</p>
<ul>
<li>requests per second;</li>
<li>requests per day;</li>
<li>transactions per month;</li>
<li>queue volume;</li>
<li>data stored;</li>
<li>active users;</li>
<li>customer organizations;</li>
<li>external integrations;</li>
<li>batch duration;</li>
<li>peak concurrency;</li>
<li>availability target;</li>
<li>latency objective;</li>
<li>deployment frequency.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Large-scale example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The platform processes approximately 80,000 requests per minute at peak and 250 million asynchronous events per day.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Smaller but complex example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>We serve approximately 400 enterprise customers. Request volume is moderate, but each customer has complex permission, billing, and data-retention rules.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Regulated example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Transaction volume is modest, but every data change must be traceable and retained for regulatory reporting.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Early-stage example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>We currently serve fewer than 100 customers. The challenge is not traffic yet; it is designing boundaries and operational practices that let the product grow safely.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Honest modest scale can be attractive.</p>
<p>Not every backend engineer wants to operate enormous distributed systems.</p>
<h1>Explain the primary technical challenge</h1>
<p>Backend roles commonly emphasize one or more of these areas.</p>
<h2>API and product development</h2>
<p>The engineer primarily builds APIs and business workflows for web or mobile products.</p>
<h2>Data modeling</h2>
<p>The role centers on schema design, data integrity, migrations, query behavior, and analytical or operational data.</p>
<h2>Distributed systems</h2>
<p>The work involves concurrency, service boundaries, messaging, consistency, fault tolerance, and partial failure.</p>
<h2>Integrations</h2>
<p>The engineer connects external providers, partner APIs, payment services, identity platforms, or customer systems.</p>
<h2>Reliability</h2>
<p>The role focuses on incidents, observability, resilience, performance, and production operation.</p>
<h2>Legacy modernization</h2>
<p>The engineer incrementally replaces or restructures older systems without interrupting customers.</p>
<h2>Platform capabilities</h2>
<p>The work provides authentication, billing, permissions, search, notifications, files, workflow orchestration, or other shared services.</p>
<h2>Regulated correctness</h2>
<p>The domain requires strong auditability, privacy, security, traceability, or financial correctness.</p>
<p>Name the primary focus.</p>
<p>A candidate who wants database-intensive work may not enjoy spending most of the week integrating third-party APIs. A distributed-systems specialist may not want a role centered on CRUD product features. Neither candidate is wrong.</p>
<h1>Explain data ownership</h1>
<p>Backend engineers need to know what data responsibilities accompany the role.</p>
<p>Clarify whether they will own:</p>
<ul>
<li>schema design;</li>
<li>migrations;</li>
<li>production data correction;</li>
<li>data retention;</li>
<li>deletion;</li>
<li>audit trails;</li>
<li>backups;</li>
<li>recovery;</li>
<li>privacy controls;</li>
<li>data contracts;</li>
<li>analytics models;</li>
<li>customer exports;</li>
<li>reconciliation.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The team owns the PostgreSQL schemas supporting subscriptions, invoices, and payment attempts. Engineers design migrations and backfills, while the platform team owns database infrastructure, backups, and failover.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This is clearer than saying:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Experience with databases required.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Explain API consumers</h1>
<p>An API designed for one internal web client differs from an API used by hundreds of external developers.</p>
<p>State who consumes the system:</p>
<ul>
<li>internal frontend;</li>
<li>native mobile applications;</li>
<li>external customers;</li>
<li>partners;</li>
<li>internal services;</li>
<li>public developer ecosystem;</li>
<li>embedded devices;</li>
<li>batch systems.</li>
</ul>
<p>Clarify whether the role includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>versioning;</li>
<li>compatibility;</li>
<li>rate limits;</li>
<li>developer documentation;</li>
<li>SDKs;</li>
<li>webhooks;</li>
<li>partner support;</li>
<li>deprecation planning.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The APIs are consumed by our web application, two mobile clients, and approximately 60 partner integrations. Backward compatibility and clear deprecation plans are therefore important parts of the work.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Describe consistency and correctness requirements</h1>
<p>Some systems may tolerate eventual consistency.</p>
<p>Others cannot safely process the same action twice.</p>
<p>Clarify whether the work includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>payments;</li>
<li>inventory;</li>
<li>identity;</li>
<li>entitlements;</li>
<li>scheduling;</li>
<li>healthcare;</li>
<li>financial records;</li>
<li>audit logs;</li>
<li>messaging;</li>
<li>synchronization.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Inventory reservations must remain correct across retries and concurrent requests. The team uses database constraints, idempotency keys, and reconciliation jobs rather than relying only on application-level checks.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This attracts engineers interested in correctness and gives candidates a meaningful discussion topic.</p>
<h1>Publish the on-call expectations</h1>
<p>On-call is a material working condition.</p>
<p>It should not be revealed during the final interview.</p>
<p>Include:</p>
<ul>
<li>whether the role participates;</li>
<li>rotation size;</li>
<li>frequency;</li>
<li>coverage hours;</li>
<li>expected response time;</li>
<li>typical page volume;</li>
<li>overnight frequency;</li>
<li>escalation support;</li>
<li>compensation or recovery time;</li>
<li>when new hires join the rotation.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Engineers join an eight-person on-call rotation after approximately three months of onboarding. The primary rotation lasts one week every eight weeks. The team receives approximately two actionable pages per month, with overnight alerts occurring a few times per quarter. A secondary engineer and engineering manager are available for escalation, and employees receive recovery time after significant overnight incidents.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Another honest example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The current on-call load is higher than we want. The team receives several pages per week, primarily from the legacy importer, and reducing that load is one of the first responsibilities of this role.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The second example may be challenging, but it allows the right candidate to opt in knowingly.</p>
<h1>Do not hide support responsibilities</h1>
<p>Some backend roles include:</p>
<ul>
<li>investigating customer data;</li>
<li>supporting partner integrations;</li>
<li>reviewing failed jobs;</li>
<li>joining customer calls;</li>
<li>debugging production configuration;</li>
<li>answering internal technical questions.</li>
</ul>
<p>State how much time this consumes.</p>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Engineers spend approximately 10% of their time helping support investigate complex integration or data issues. The support team owns customer communication, while engineers provide technical diagnosis and fixes.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This prevents “backend ownership” from becoming unexpected frontline support.</p>
<h1>Explain security expectations</h1>
<p>Backend developers often handle:</p>
<ul>
<li>authentication;</li>
<li>authorization;</li>
<li>secrets;</li>
<li>personal data;</li>
<li>financial data;</li>
<li>file uploads;</li>
<li>administrative actions;</li>
<li>integration credentials;</li>
<li>audit records.</li>
</ul>
<p>Relevant responsibilities may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>validating input;</li>
<li>enforcing authorization server-side;</li>
<li>applying least privilege;</li>
<li>protecting secrets;</li>
<li>avoiding sensitive logs;</li>
<li>reviewing dependencies;</li>
<li>designing secure defaults;</li>
<li>supporting vulnerability remediation;</li>
<li>participating in threat modeling.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The backend developer is responsible for all application security.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Security should be shared and supported.</p>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Engineers are responsible for secure implementation and server-side authorization within their services. The application-security team provides design review, tooling, and incident support for higher-risk changes.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Explain performance expectations</h1>
<p>Performance can mean several things:</p>
<ul>
<li>low latency;</li>
<li>high throughput;</li>
<li>predictable batch completion;</li>
<li>database efficiency;</li>
<li>cost control;</li>
<li>memory usage;</li>
<li>fast startup;</li>
<li>mobile-network compatibility;</li>
<li>external API rate limits.</li>
</ul>
<p>Name the relevant one.</p>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Most product APIs target a p95 latency below 300 milliseconds. Large customer exports are asynchronous and are expected to complete within 20 minutes without affecting interactive traffic.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Candidates can then discuss relevant engineering judgment.</p>
<h1>Explain reliability expectations</h1>
<p>Useful information includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>service-level objectives;</li>
<li>availability targets;</li>
<li>error budgets;</li>
<li>incident frequency;</li>
<li>recovery expectations;</li>
<li>disaster recovery;</li>
<li>deployment strategy;</li>
<li>rollback capability.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>The payment API has a 99.95% availability target. Releases use progressive rollout and can be rolled back independently. The team reviews every customer-impacting incident and tracks follow-up work to completion.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Do not invent mature reliability practices if they do not exist.</p>
<p>You may instead say:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>We do not yet have formal service-level objectives. Defining meaningful indicators and improving alert quality will be part of this role.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Explain how technical decisions are made</h1>
<p>Backend candidates want to know whether they can influence architecture.</p>
<p>Describe:</p>
<ul>
<li>written proposals;</li>
<li>architecture review;</li>
<li>team discussion;</li>
<li>staff-engineer approval;</li>
<li>manager authority;</li>
<li>experimentation;</li>
<li>production validation.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Engineers write short design proposals for changes involving new services, shared schemas, or public APIs. The owning team makes the decision after review from affected teams and a staff engineer. We prefer reversible decisions and incremental migrations.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This is more useful than saying the company values “ownership.”</p>
<h1>Explain the relationship with infrastructure</h1>
<p>Clarify whether backend developers:</p>
<ul>
<li>write application code only;</li>
<li>own service configuration;</li>
<li>define infrastructure as code;</li>
<li>deploy independently;</li>
<li>manage Kubernetes;</li>
<li>rotate through platform support;</li>
<li>share observability responsibilities.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Backend engineers own service containers, runtime configuration, dashboards, alerts, and deployment manifests. The platform team owns clusters, networking, identity infrastructure, and shared CI/CD systems.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This helps candidates understand whether “backend” includes significant platform work.</p>
<h1>Compensation</h1>
<p>Publish a real compensation range.</p>
<p>Include:</p>
<ul>
<li>currency;</li>
<li>base salary;</li>
<li>bonus;</li>
<li>equity;</li>
<li>geographic adjustment;</li>
<li>contractor rate where relevant;</li>
<li>level.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Base salary: $135,000–$175,000 USD
Annual bonus: Up to 10%
Equity: Included for eligible employees
Compensation varies by role level and approved work location.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Competitive salary based on experience.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>That phrase provides no decision-making value.</p>

<h1>Remote and location expectations</h1>
<p>State:</p>
<ul>
<li>permitted countries or states;</li>
<li>required time-zone overlap;</li>
<li>office attendance;</li>
<li>travel;</li>
<li>relocation;</li>
<li>employment structure;</li>
<li>work authorization;</li>
<li>whether remote status is permanent.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Remote example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>This role is remote within the United States and Canada. Team members must maintain at least four hours of overlap with US Eastern Time. The team meets in person twice per year, with travel covered by the company.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Hybrid example</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>This role is hybrid in Atlanta, with required office attendance on Tuesdays and Thursdays.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Do not advertise a role as remote when the employee must live near an office or may be required to return later.</p>
<h1>Interview process</h1>
<p>A strong backend interview process should evaluate the work the engineer will actually perform.</p>
<h2>Stage 1: introductory conversation — 30 minutes</h2>
<p>Meet [recruiter or hiring manager].</p>
<p>Discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>role scope;</li>
<li>compensation;</li>
<li>location;</li>
<li>candidate experience;</li>
<li>practical constraints;</li>
<li>interview process;</li>
<li>candidate questions.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Stage 2: systems and experience discussion — 60 minutes</h2>
<p>Ask the candidate to walk through one or two backend systems they built or operated.</p>
<p>Evaluate:</p>
<ul>
<li>their personal contribution;</li>
<li>architecture;</li>
<li>data model;</li>
<li>API design;</li>
<li>testing;</li>
<li>deployment;</li>
<li>observability;</li>
<li>failure behavior;</li>
<li>production learning.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Stage 3: practical technical session — 60 to 120 minutes</h2>
<p>Choose a format that resembles the work:</p>
<ul>
<li>API and data-model design;</li>
<li>debugging an existing service;</li>
<li>reviewing a pull request;</li>
<li>designing a migration;</li>
<li>extending a small application;</li>
<li>discussing a realistic incident;</li>
<li>paid take-home assignment.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Stage 4: production and reliability discussion — 45 to 60 minutes</h2>
<p>Discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>incidents;</li>
<li>observability;</li>
<li>migrations;</li>
<li>retries;</li>
<li>idempotency;</li>
<li>on-call;</li>
<li>recovery;</li>
<li>security;</li>
<li>operational ownership.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Stage 5: team conversation — 45 minutes</h2>
<p>Meet:</p>
<ul>
<li>future engineers;</li>
<li>product or frontend partners;</li>
<li>platform or data partners;</li>
<li>manager.</li>
</ul>
<p>Discuss collaboration and allow substantial candidate questions.</p>
<h2>Decision</h2>
<p>We aim to provide a final decision or clear update within [number] business days.</p>
<p>Publish the process in advance.</p>
<p>Do not add surprise stages unless genuinely necessary.</p>
<h1>What to evaluate during résumé screening</h1>
<p>A backend résumé should show more than a list of technologies.</p>
<p>Look for evidence of:</p>
<ul>
<li>production ownership;</li>
<li>API design;</li>
<li>schema design;</li>
<li>data migration;</li>
<li>debugging;</li>
<li>reliability;</li>
<li>performance;</li>
<li>security;</li>
<li>testing;</li>
<li>deployment;</li>
<li>incident response;</li>
<li>measurable outcomes;</li>
<li>collaboration.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Strong evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Redesigned the subscription-renewal workflow using idempotency keys, database constraints, and reconciliation jobs, reducing duplicate charges from an average of 14 per month to zero.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Weak evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Responsible for payment APIs.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Strong evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Reduced p95 response time from 1.4 seconds to 310 milliseconds by removing repeated database queries and redesigning two high-volume indexes.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Weak evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Improved application performance.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Strong evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Led a backward-compatible migration of 60 million account records using staged backfills, dual reads, and validation reports.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Weak evidence</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Worked on database migrations.</p>
</blockquote>
<h1>Do not overvalue famous employers</h1>
<p>A recognizable company name may provide context.</p>
<p>It does not prove:</p>
<ul>
<li>ownership;</li>
<li>technical depth;</li>
<li>production judgment;</li>
<li>communication;</li>
<li>relevance to your system.</li>
</ul>
<p>A developer at a smaller company may have broader end-to-end responsibility than someone at a large organization who worked within a narrow component.</p>
<p>Evaluate the work.</p>
<h1>Do not reject transferable language experience automatically</h1>
<p>Backend fundamentals often transfer across languages.</p>
<p>A strong Java engineer may become productive in Kotlin or C# quickly.</p>
<p>A strong Python engineer may adapt to TypeScript.</p>
<p>A Go engineer may bring valuable concurrency and service-design experience into Rust.</p>
<p>Prioritize:</p>
<ul>
<li>system design;</li>
<li>API judgment;</li>
<li>databases;</li>
<li>testing;</li>
<li>debugging;</li>
<li>production operation;</li>
<li>language fundamentals;</li>
<li>learning ability.</li>
</ul>
<p>Require exact language experience only when the ramp risk genuinely justifies it.</p>
<h1>Screen for database competence</h1>
<p>Backend engineers should be able to reason about data, not merely call an ORM.</p>
<p>Useful screening areas include:</p>
<ul>
<li>schema design;</li>
<li>normalization and denormalization;</li>
<li>indexes;</li>
<li>constraints;</li>
<li>transactions;</li>
<li>isolation;</li>
<li>query plans;</li>
<li>migrations;</li>
<li>locking;</li>
<li>backfills;</li>
<li>retention;</li>
<li>deletion;</li>
<li>consistency.</li>
</ul>
<p>A candidate does not need to be a database administrator.</p>
<p>They should understand how application decisions affect data correctness and performance.</p>
<h1>Database screening questions</h1>
<h2>“Tell me about a schema you designed.”</h2>
<p>Listen for:</p>
<ul>
<li>entity boundaries;</li>
<li>relationships;</li>
<li>constraints;</li>
<li>access patterns;</li>
<li>future changes;</li>
<li>trade-offs.</li>
</ul>
<h2>“Tell me about a slow query you investigated.”</h2>
<p>Listen for:</p>
<ul>
<li>measurement;</li>
<li>execution plans;</li>
<li>indexes;</li>
<li>row estimates;</li>
<li>repeated queries;</li>
<li>result size;</li>
<li>caching;</li>
<li>verification.</li>
</ul>
<h2>“Describe a risky production migration.”</h2>
<p>Listen for:</p>
<ul>
<li>backward compatibility;</li>
<li>staging;</li>
<li>backfill;</li>
<li>validation;</li>
<li>rollback;</li>
<li>locking;</li>
<li>consumer coordination.</li>
</ul>
<h2>“When would you use a database constraint instead of application validation?”</h2>
<p>Strong candidates understand that important invariants often deserve enforcement close to the data.</p>
<h1>Screen for API judgment</h1>
<p>Evaluate whether candidates can design interfaces that remain understandable and maintainable.</p>
<p>Discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>resource boundaries;</li>
<li>validation;</li>
<li>error responses;</li>
<li>authorization;</li>
<li>pagination;</li>
<li>filtering;</li>
<li>versioning;</li>
<li>idempotency;</li>
<li>retries;</li>
<li>rate limiting;</li>
<li>compatibility;</li>
<li>observability.</li>
</ul>
<h2>API screening question</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Design an endpoint that creates a payment or inventory reservation. What happens when the client retries after timing out?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This reveals whether the candidate thinks beyond the happy path.</p>
<h1>Screen for failure handling</h1>
<p>Backend systems fail through:</p>
<ul>
<li>network timeouts;</li>
<li>duplicate delivery;</li>
<li>dependency outages;</li>
<li>partial completion;</li>
<li>invalid input;</li>
<li>stale data;</li>
<li>lock contention;</li>
<li>queue backlog;</li>
<li>expired credentials;</li>
<li>resource exhaustion.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>What can fail in this design, and how would the system recover?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Strong candidates do not assume that every request completes once and in order.</p>
<h1>Screen for idempotency knowledge</h1>
<p>Idempotency is especially relevant to:</p>
<ul>
<li>payments;</li>
<li>webhooks;</li>
<li>jobs;</li>
<li>messaging;</li>
<li>inventory;</li>
<li>provisioning;</li>
<li>integrations.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>How would you make this operation safe to retry?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Good answers may discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>idempotency keys;</li>
<li>unique constraints;</li>
<li>operation records;</li>
<li>deduplication;</li>
<li>deterministic identifiers;</li>
<li>transactional boundaries;</li>
<li>replay behavior.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Screen for operational experience</h1>
<p>The gap between building a service and operating one is significant.</p>
<p>Ask candidates about:</p>
<ul>
<li>a production incident;</li>
<li>an unexpected migration result;</li>
<li>alert quality;</li>
<li>log design;</li>
<li>rollback;</li>
<li>dependency failure;</li>
<li>capacity problem;</li>
<li>customer-impacting defect.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Incident question</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>Tell me about a production incident you personally investigated. How did you discover it, contain it, identify the cause, and prevent recurrence?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Look for:</p>
<ol>
<li>detection;</li>
<li>customer or business impact;</li>
<li>mitigation;</li>
<li>evidence gathering;</li>
<li>root cause;</li>
<li>follow-up safeguards;</li>
<li>communication;</li>
<li>learning.</li>
</ol>
<p>Avoid rewarding dramatic storytelling over disciplined investigation.</p>
<h1>Screen for observability judgment</h1>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>What would you monitor for this service?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Good answers may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>request rate;</li>
<li>latency;</li>
<li>error rate;</li>
<li>saturation;</li>
<li>queue depth;</li>
<li>job age;</li>
<li>retry volume;</li>
<li>dependency failures;</li>
<li>business outcomes;</li>
<li>data reconciliation.</li>
</ul>
<p>Strong engineers distinguish between:</p>
<ul>
<li>logs;</li>
<li>metrics;</li>
<li>traces;</li>
<li>alerts;</li>
<li>dashboards;</li>
<li>business-level indicators.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Screen for security fundamentals</h1>
<p>Useful topics include:</p>
<ul>
<li>authentication;</li>
<li>authorization;</li>
<li>tenant isolation;</li>
<li>input validation;</li>
<li>injection;</li>
<li>secrets;</li>
<li>data exposure;</li>
<li>rate limiting;</li>
<li>audit logging;</li>
<li>sensitive logs;</li>
<li>dependency risk.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Security screening question</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>A user can belong to several organizations. How would you ensure they can access only records belonging to organizations where they have an active membership?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Look for server-side authorization, careful data access, testing, and denial by default.</p>
<h1>Screen for migration judgment</h1>
<p>Backend work frequently involves changing live systems.</p>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>How would you replace a heavily used API or database column without taking the product down?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Strong answers may discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>compatibility;</li>
<li>additive changes;</li>
<li>dual reads or writes;</li>
<li>backfills;</li>
<li>validation;</li>
<li>staged rollout;</li>
<li>feature flags;</li>
<li>deprecation;</li>
<li>monitoring;</li>
<li>cleanup.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Screen for appropriate simplicity</h1>
<p>Not every backend problem needs:</p>
<ul>
<li>microservices;</li>
<li>Kafka;</li>
<li>event sourcing;</li>
<li>Kubernetes;</li>
<li>distributed transactions;</li>
<li>several databases.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>When would you keep this inside the existing application instead of creating a new service?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Strong candidates can justify both separation and consolidation.</p>
<p>Complexity should solve a real problem.</p>
<h1>Screen for communication</h1>
<p>Backend developers must explain technical decisions to:</p>
<ul>
<li>frontend teams;</li>
<li>mobile engineers;</li>
<li>product managers;</li>
<li>support;</li>
<li>security;</li>
<li>platform teams;</li>
<li>customers or partners.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask candidates to explain a system at two levels:</p>
<ol>
<li>to another backend engineer;</li>
<li>to a nontechnical product partner.</li>
</ol>
<p>This reveals whether they can adapt communication to the audience.</p>
<h1>Screen for ownership without encouraging hero culture</h1>
<p>Ownership means:</p>
<ul>
<li>understanding the outcome;</li>
<li>identifying risk;</li>
<li>communicating blockers;</li>
<li>following through;</li>
<li>verifying production behavior;</li>
<li>improving the system after failure.</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not mean:</p>
<ul>
<li>working alone;</li>
<li>being available constantly;</li>
<li>accepting unlimited scope;</li>
<li>hiding problems until they are solved;</li>
<li>becoming the only person who understands a service.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Tell me about a system you owned. How did you ensure other people could support it?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Look for documentation, review, runbooks, knowledge sharing, and maintainable design.</p>
<h1>Job-relevant practical exercises</h1>
<p>Good backend assessments resemble actual work.</p>
<h2>API extension exercise</h2>
<p>Provide a small service and ask the candidate to add:</p>
<ul>
<li>one endpoint;</li>
<li>validation;</li>
<li>persistence;</li>
<li>tests;</li>
<li>error handling.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Debugging exercise</h2>
<p>Provide:</p>
<ul>
<li>a failing test;</li>
<li>misleading symptom;</li>
<li>logs;</li>
<li>a small data or concurrency issue.</li>
</ul>
<p>Observe the investigation process.</p>
<h2>Code-review exercise</h2>
<p>Ask the candidate to review a small pull request containing:</p>
<ul>
<li>missing authorization;</li>
<li>repeated queries;</li>
<li>unclear transaction boundaries;</li>
<li>inadequate error handling;</li>
<li>brittle tests.</li>
</ul>
<p>This reveals judgment and communication.</p>
<h2>Data-model exercise</h2>
<p>Present a business domain and ask the candidate to model:</p>
<ul>
<li>entities;</li>
<li>relationships;</li>
<li>constraints;</li>
<li>expected queries;</li>
<li>future changes.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Migration exercise</h2>
<p>Ask the candidate to plan a safe change to:</p>
<ul>
<li>schema;</li>
<li>API;</li>
<li>event contract;</li>
<li>service boundary.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Incident exercise</h2>
<p>Present a simplified production incident and ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>what they would check;</li>
<li>how they would reduce impact;</li>
<li>what information they need;</li>
<li>what follow-up changes they would make.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Avoid oversized take-home assignments</h1>
<p>A backend take-home should generally take no more than two or three hours.</p>
<p>State:</p>
<ul>
<li>expected duration;</li>
<li>evaluation criteria;</li>
<li>required scope;</li>
<li>optional scope;</li>
<li>allowed tools;</li>
<li>AI policy;</li>
<li>submission format;</li>
<li>review timeline.</li>
</ul>
<p>Pay for substantial assignments.</p>
<p>Offer a live alternative when possible.</p>
<p>Never ask candidates to solve a real production problem your company intends to use.</p>
<h1>AI-tool policy</h1>
<p>Candidates may use AI tools in ordinary engineering work.</p>
<p>Define whether they are permitted during assessments.</p>
<p>One possible policy:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>AI assistants may be used during the practical exercise. Candidates remain responsible for understanding, testing, securing, and explaining all submitted work. The follow-up discussion may include modifying requirements or debugging the solution.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Another possible policy:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>AI assistance is not permitted during this exercise because we are evaluating unaided backend fundamentals. Official documentation and ordinary web search are allowed.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Whichever policy you choose:</p>
<ul>
<li>state it before the exercise;</li>
<li>apply it consistently;</li>
<li>avoid secret expectations;</li>
<li>evaluate role-relevant ability.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Interview scorecard</h1>
<p>Use the same core criteria for every candidate.</p>
<p>Possible competencies include:</p>
<h2>Backend fundamentals</h2>
<p>Understands APIs, application structure, errors, concurrency, and service behavior.</p>
<h2>Data modeling</h2>
<p>Designs clear schemas and protects important invariants.</p>
<h2>Reliability</h2>
<p>Thinks about failure, retries, recovery, and production impact.</p>
<h2>Testing</h2>
<p>Chooses tests based on behavior and risk.</p>
<h2>Security</h2>
<p>Applies authentication, authorization, validation, and least privilege appropriately.</p>
<h2>Operational judgment</h2>
<p>Can deploy, observe, debug, and improve production systems.</p>
<h2>Technical design</h2>
<p>Chooses reasonable boundaries and explains trade-offs.</p>
<h2>Communication</h2>
<p>Explains decisions clearly and collaborates constructively.</p>
<h2>Ownership</h2>
<p>Follows work through release and production verification.</p>
<h2>Role level</h2>
<p>Demonstrates scope and independence appropriate to the position.</p>
<p>Score observable evidence.</p>
<p>Avoid vague judgments such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>not senior enough;</li>
<li>did not feel technical;</li>
<li>not a culture fit;</li>
<li>lacked confidence;</li>
<li>seemed too quiet.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Calibrate expectations by level</h1>
<h1>Junior Backend Developer</h1>
<p>May be expected to:</p>
<ul>
<li>complete clearly scoped changes;</li>
<li>work within established patterns;</li>
<li>write basic tests;</li>
<li>use the database safely;</li>
<li>debug with guidance;</li>
<li>ask for help appropriately;</li>
<li>participate in code review.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not expect independent distributed-system architecture.</p>
<h1>Mid-Level Backend Developer</h1>
<p>May be expected to:</p>
<ul>
<li>own features independently;</li>
<li>design ordinary APIs and schemas;</li>
<li>debug routine production problems;</li>
<li>handle manageable ambiguity;</li>
<li>contribute to migrations and operational work;</li>
<li>communicate risk.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Senior Backend Developer</h1>
<p>May be expected to:</p>
<ul>
<li>own complex systems;</li>
<li>lead migrations;</li>
<li>anticipate failure;</li>
<li>mentor engineers;</li>
<li>improve reliability;</li>
<li>influence architecture;</li>
<li>coordinate across teams;</li>
<li>make sound trade-offs under ambiguity.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Staff Backend Engineer</h1>
<p>May be expected to:</p>
<ul>
<li>lead initiatives spanning several teams;</li>
<li>establish technical direction;</li>
<li>solve cross-system problems;</li>
<li>create migration strategies;</li>
<li>reduce organizational risk;</li>
<li>influence without direct authority;</li>
<li>improve engineering standards broadly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not use one interview bar for every level.</p>
<h1>Questions candidates may ask you</h1>
<p>Be prepared to answer:</p>
<ul>
<li>What would I own?</li>
<li>What does the architecture look like?</li>
<li>What is the main technical challenge?</li>
<li>How much traffic and data does the system handle?</li>
<li>How often do you deploy?</li>
<li>Who owns infrastructure?</li>
<li>How does on-call work?</li>
<li>How often does it page?</li>
<li>What was the most recent major incident?</li>
<li>What is the largest source of technical debt?</li>
<li>How are schema changes handled?</li>
<li>How are architectural decisions made?</li>
<li>What would I work on first?</li>
<li>What does success look like after 90 days?</li>
<li>How much of the role involves support or integrations?</li>
<li>Why is the position open?</li>
</ul>
<p>Weak or evasive answers can cause experienced candidates to withdraw.</p>
<h1>Common backend job-description mistakes</h1>
<h2>Listing tools instead of describing work</h2>
<p>Candidates learn the stack but not the problem.</p>
<h2>Claiming “massive scale” without evidence</h2>
<p>The phrase becomes meaningless.</p>
<h2>Hiding technical debt</h2>
<p>Candidates discover it during onboarding and lose trust.</p>
<h2>Omitting on-call</h2>
<p>A major working condition appears late.</p>
<h2>Requiring every language and platform</h2>
<p>The candidate pool becomes unnecessarily narrow.</p>
<h2>Combining backend, DevOps, security, data, and support into one role</h2>
<p>The scope becomes unrealistic.</p>
<h2>Calling a primarily integration role a distributed-systems role</h2>
<p>Candidates join expecting the wrong work.</p>
<h2>Using algorithm puzzles for an integration and database role</h2>
<p>The process measures interview preparation rather than job performance.</p>
<h2>Ignoring database skill</h2>
<p>The role depends on data, but the interview tests only application syntax.</p>
<h2>Evaluating construction without operation</h2>
<p>Candidates can build demos but may not understand production responsibility.</p>
<h2>Requiring internet-scale experience for modest systems</h2>
<p>Excellent candidates self-reject unnecessarily.</p>
<h2>Hiding customer support work</h2>
<p>Engineers discover unexpected responsibilities after joining.</p>
<h2>Treating every senior candidate as an architect</h2>
<p>Strong senior engineers may specialize in execution, reliability, or domain depth without wanting broad architecture ownership.</p>
<h1>Phrases to remove</h1>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<ul>
<li>rockstar backend developer;</li>
<li>coding ninja;</li>
<li>high-performance individual;</li>
<li>fast-paced environment;</li>
<li>wear many hats;</li>
<li>massive scale;</li>
<li>cutting-edge microservices;</li>
<li>world-class architecture;</li>
<li>competitive salary;</li>
<li>hit the ground running;</li>
<li>must thrive under pressure;</li>
<li>move fast and break things;</li>
<li>no task is beneath you.</li>
</ul>
<p>Replace them with facts.</p>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Work on high-scale systems</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Operate services processing approximately 25 million requests per day, with peak traffic near 1,200 requests per second.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Wear many hats</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The role is approximately 70% application development, 20% production and reliability work, and 10% technical support for partner integrations.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Instead of:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Fast-paced environment</p>
</blockquote>
<p>write:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Product priorities are reviewed every two weeks, though urgent customer-impacting incidents may occasionally change planned work.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1>Hiring-manager alignment checklist</h1>
<p>Before opening the role, the hiring manager should be able to answer:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which system or domain will this person own?</li>
<li>What is the first major project?</li>
<li>What level of independence is required?</li>
<li>Which skills are truly mandatory?</li>
<li>Which technologies can be learned?</li>
<li>Who owns infrastructure?</li>
<li>Who owns database operations?</li>
<li>How much on-call work exists?</li>
<li>What support load exists?</li>
<li>Which technical debt will the person inherit?</li>
<li>What would make the hire successful after six months?</li>
<li>What evidence will each interview stage collect?</li>
<li>Who makes the final decision?</li>
<li>Is onboarding ready?</li>
</ul>
<h1>Résumé-screening checklist</h1>
<p>Look for evidence of:</p>
<ul>
<li>backend system ownership;</li>
<li>APIs;</li>
<li>databases;</li>
<li>migrations;</li>
<li>tests;</li>
<li>deployment;</li>
<li>observability;</li>
<li>incidents;</li>
<li>reliability;</li>
<li>security;</li>
<li>performance;</li>
<li>measurable results;</li>
<li>communication;</li>
<li>collaboration.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not require:</p>
<ul>
<li>every technology in your stack;</li>
<li>a specific degree;</li>
<li>a famous employer;</li>
<li>public repositories;</li>
<li>internet-scale traffic;</li>
<li>years beyond what the role requires.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Interviewer checklist</h1>
<p>Before the interview:</p>
<ul>
<li>understand the assigned competency;</li>
<li>review the role level;</li>
<li>use consistent questions;</li>
<li>understand the system context;</li>
<li>know the permitted tools;</li>
<li>understand accommodations.</li>
</ul>
<p>During the interview:</p>
<ul>
<li>explain the format;</li>
<li>gather evidence;</li>
<li>ask follow-up questions;</li>
<li>distinguish team work from personal contribution;</li>
<li>avoid unrelated trivia;</li>
<li>allow candidate questions.</li>
</ul>
<p>After the interview:</p>
<ul>
<li>submit feedback independently;</li>
<li>cite specific evidence;</li>
<li>compare against the rubric;</li>
<li>distinguish concerns from preferences;</li>
<li>avoid vague culture-fit language;</li>
<li>respond promptly.</li>
</ul>
<h1>A shorter ready-to-publish example</h1>
<h2>Senior Backend Developer — Payments Platform</h2>
<p><strong>Location:</strong> Remote within the United States
<strong>Compensation:</strong> $150,000–$185,000 base, plus equity
<strong>Employment type:</strong> Full-time
<strong>Reports to:</strong> Engineering Manager</p>
<h3>About us</h3>
<p>Acme builds subscription-management software used by more than 8,000 online businesses. Our payment platform processes approximately 20 million billing and renewal events each month across several external payment providers.</p>
<p>We are hiring a Senior Backend Developer to own payment retries, reconciliation, and provider integrations.</p>
<p>The first six months will focus on replacing an older retry workflow, improving transaction visibility, and reducing manual reconciliation.</p>
<h3>What you will do</h3>
<ul>
<li>Build and operate TypeScript and PostgreSQL services.</li>
<li>Own payment-retry and reconciliation workflows.</li>
<li>Design idempotent APIs and background jobs.</li>
<li>Evolve schemas through safe migrations and backfills.</li>
<li>Improve observability, alerting, and incident response.</li>
<li>Work with product, support, finance, and platform teams.</li>
<li>Review code and mentor other engineers.</li>
<li>Join a seven-person on-call rotation after onboarding.</li>
</ul>
<h3>What we are looking for</h3>
<ul>
<li>Experience building and operating production backend systems.</li>
<li>Strong experience in TypeScript, Java, Go, C#, or a comparable language.</li>
<li>Strong relational-database fundamentals.</li>
<li>Experience with APIs, background processing, and asynchronous failure.</li>
<li>Practical testing and debugging skills.</li>
<li>Clear written communication.</li>
<li>Experience supporting software after release.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Nice to have</h3>
<ul>
<li>Payments or financial-system experience.</li>
<li>Kafka, RabbitMQ, or another messaging system.</li>
<li>AWS.</li>
<li>OpenTelemetry.</li>
<li>Multi-tenant SaaS systems.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Our stack</h3>
<p>TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Redis, Kafka, AWS, GitHub Actions, OpenTelemetry, and Grafana.</p>
<p>The core billing service is well tested. An older provider-import module has limited observability and creates most of the team&#39;s current on-call load. Replacing it is part of this role.</p>
<h3>Scale and reliability</h3>
<ul>
<li>Approximately 20 million billing events per month</li>
<li>Peak throughput of roughly 400 events per second</li>
<li>99.95% availability target for payment APIs</li>
<li>Progressive production rollout with automated rollback</li>
<li>Monthly incident review for customer-impacting failures</li>
</ul>
<h3>On-call</h3>
<p>The rotation includes seven engineers. Each engineer is primary for one week approximately every seven weeks. The team receives about two actionable pages per month, with overnight alerts occurring a few times per quarter. A secondary engineer is always available, and recovery time is provided after significant overnight incidents.</p>
<h3>Interview process</h3>
<ol>
<li>30-minute hiring-manager conversation.</li>
<li>60-minute discussion about a backend system you built and operated.</li>
<li>90-minute collaborative API, data-model, and failure-handling exercise.</li>
<li>45-minute production and incident discussion.</li>
<li>45-minute team conversation.</li>
</ol>
<p>No algorithm puzzles and no unpaid take-home assignment.</p>
<p>We aim to provide a decision within ten business days.</p>
<h3>Benefits</h3>
<ul>
<li>Medical, dental, and vision coverage</li>
<li>25 days of annual paid leave</li>
<li>Twelve weeks of paid parental leave</li>
<li>$2,000 annual learning budget</li>
<li>Laptop and home-office stipend</li>
<li>401(k) matching</li>
<li>Two annual company gatherings with travel covered</li>
</ul>
<h3>How to apply</h3>
<p>Apply through [link] and tell us briefly about a backend system you owned after it reached production.</p>
<p>We provide reasonable accommodations throughout the interview process.</p>
<h1>The takeaway</h1>
<p>Backend developers evaluate opportunities by understanding the systems they will inherit, the problems they will solve, and the operational responsibility they will carry.</p>
<p>A strong backend job description should clearly explain:</p>
<ol>
<li>What the system does.</li>
<li>Who depends on it.</li>
<li>How much traffic or data it handles.</li>
<li>Which technical challenge matters most.</li>
<li>What the engineer will own.</li>
<li>Which data and API responsibilities are included.</li>
<li>Where the infrastructure boundary sits.</li>
<li>What technical debt exists.</li>
<li>How often production support or on-call occurs.</li>
<li>What success looks like after joining.</li>
<li>How candidates will be evaluated.</li>
<li>How much the role pays.</li>
</ol>
<p>The strongest postings do not pretend the system is perfect.</p>
<p>They communicate the opportunity honestly.</p>
<p>A backend engineer may be excited to modernize a legacy importer, stabilize a payment platform, redesign a schema, reduce queue failures, or build a new service. The candidate only needs enough information to recognize that the challenge is one they want.</p>
<p>Describe the real system.</p>
<p>Publish real scale.</p>
<p>Name the actual problem.</p>
<p>Explain ownership.</p>
<p>Disclose on-call.</p>
<p>Interview for databases, failure handling, migrations, security, reliability, and production judgment rather than memorized trivia.</p>
<p>That is how you attract backend developers who understand not only how to create services, but how to operate and improve them after real users begin depending on the result.</p>
