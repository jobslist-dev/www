No Webcam, No Surveillance: How We Respect You During an Assessment

The JobsList.dev Team·24 Juli 2026·3 min read

If you've taken an online assessment before, you may have been asked to turn on your webcam, share your screen, install a lockdown browser, or sit still while an algorithm decided whether your eye movements looked "suspicious." We don't do any of that, on purpose. This article explains what our assessments do and don't collect, and why we're confident we can keep things fair without watching you.

What we don't do

Let's be direct about what you will never encounter in a JobsList.dev assessment:

  • No webcam monitoring. We don't look at you, record you, or analyze your face or eyes.
  • No screen recording or screen sharing.
  • No lockdown or "secure" browser that takes over your machine.
  • No threatening warnings about being watched or about switching tabs.
  • No biometric data collection. We don't want it and we don't store it.

You take the assessment in your own space, on your own device, comfortably.

What we do collect — and only this

We practice data minimization. For an assessment, we keep:

  • Your email and name (so the company knows whose result it is).
  • Your answers.
  • Timing information (so the time limit is fair).
  • Lightweight, neutral notes such as whether the browser tab lost focus.

That's the list. We're not building a profile of your behavior or your face.

Why we refuse to surveil

Two reasons, and both matter.

First, it's disrespectful. Watching a professional through their webcam to make sure they don't cheat assumes the worst about them from the start. That's not how we want to treat the people companies are hoping to hire, and it's not the tone we want our platform to set.

Second, it doesn't even work. The honest truth the proctoring industry avoids: anyone determined to look something up can use their phone, off-camera. Surveillance can't stop that — so it delivers a false sense of security while making the experience hostile for everyone, especially people who are anxious, neurodivergent, or simply in a shared living space. It flags normal behavior as "suspicious" and creates false accusations. We'd rather not participate in security theater.

So how is it fair without watching me?

Fairness lives in the design, not in surveillance. Behind the scenes:

  • Everyone gets a personalized set of questions, drawn from a much larger pool and shuffled, so the test isn't something you can pass by getting answers from a friend.
  • The correct answers never reach your browser — there's nothing to "find" in the page or network traffic even if you went looking.
  • The timer is handled fairly and consistently for everyone, on our side.
  • The questions reward understanding, not memorization — they're about judgment and reasoning, which is hard to shortcut and doesn't depend on us spying on you.

You get a fair test; we get trustworthy results; nobody gets watched.

About those "tab focus" notes

We do note simple, neutral things like whether your browser tab lost focus during the assessment. Please don't stress about it. This is context for the company, never an automatic judgment. People switch tabs to grab water, answer a message, or check the time — the system does not decide that's cheating, and it will never fail you on its own. It's shown to reviewers as a plain fact, and a human uses their judgment. We'd rather be honest that we note it than pretend we don't.

The trust goes both ways

We ask you to do your own work, and in return we commit to treating you like a professional: no cameras, no intimidation, no collecting things we don't need. We think that's the relationship a good assessment should start from — and it's a deliberate choice, not an oversight. Take a breath, do your best, and know that we're not watching over your shoulder.
