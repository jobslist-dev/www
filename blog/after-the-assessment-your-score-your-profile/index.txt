3:T104f,<p>You clicked submit — now what? A lot of assessment platforms leave you staring at a &quot;thank you&quot; screen with no idea what just happened to your work. We&#39;d rather be clear. Here&#39;s exactly what happens after you finish a JobsList.dev assessment: what the company sees, what you see, and the option to keep your score and turn one assessment into an ongoing head start.</p>
<h2>Your work is graded fairly</h2>
<p>Most questions are scored automatically the moment you submit — consistently, against the same standard as everyone else. Some question types (like a written explanation, if the assessment includes one) are reviewed by a person on the company&#39;s side, so those may take a little longer to finalize. Either way, the grading is designed to be fair: partial credit where it&#39;s meaningful, normalized matching so a right answer isn&#39;t marked wrong over spacing or capitalization, and no negative marking anywhere.</p>
<h2>What the company sees</h2>
<p>The hiring team receives a clear, honest picture of how you did:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Section scores</strong>, not just a single number. This shows <em>where</em> you&#39;re strong — which is what a good hiring team actually wants, and which works in your favor if you shine in the areas that matter for the role.</li>
<li><strong>Neutral context</strong>, presented separately from your score. Anything the system noted during the assessment is shown as plain facts, never as an accusation, and it never automatically fails you. A human reads it with judgment.</li>
</ul>
<p>What the company does <strong>not</strong> get: the internal answer keys, any webcam or screen footage (there is none), or a verdict handed down by an algorithm. They get a fair result and use their judgment — the way it should be.</p>
<h2>What you see</h2>
<p>Whether you see your own score, and how much detail, is up to the company that invited you — some share the full breakdown, some share a summary, some keep it internal and follow up directly. That&#39;s their call to make about their hiring process. What&#39;s consistent on our side is that your result is handled honestly and your effort is recorded.</p>
<h2>Keep your score: claim a free profile</h2>
<p>Here&#39;s the part worth knowing: after you finish, you&#39;ll be offered a <strong>free JobsList profile to keep your score</strong>. You took the assessment through a one-time link without needing an account — but if you&#39;d like, you can claim a profile in a moment and carry that result forward. With a profile you can:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hold onto your score</strong> instead of it living only with one company.</li>
<li><strong>Keep applying</strong> to other roles on JobsList.dev with a head start.</li>
<li><strong>Build a candidate profile</strong> that shows real, demonstrated ability — not just a résumé.</li>
</ul>
<p>It&#39;s completely optional and there&#39;s no pressure. If you just wanted to complete the one assessment, you&#39;re free to close the tab and you&#39;re done.</p>
<h2>Why we offer this</h2>
<p>We think it&#39;s a little unfair that in most hiring processes, <em>you</em> do the work of proving your skills and the result vanishes into one company&#39;s system the moment you&#39;re not hired. Claiming your score flips that: the effort you invested becomes <em>yours</em>, portable, and useful for the next opportunity too. One assessment can become an ongoing advantage instead of a one-time hoop.</p>
<h2>A note on cost</h2>
<p>Assessments are <strong>free during our beta</strong> — free for you as a candidate always, and currently free for companies too as we grow. Taking one never costs you anything.</p>
<h2>What to do next</h2>
<p>If you claimed a profile, complete it — add your details and let your demonstrated skills do some of the talking as you apply. If you didn&#39;t, no problem; you&#39;ve given the company a fair, real look at what you can do, which is the whole point. Either way: nicely done, and thank you for taking the time. We built this process to respect that time — and to make the work you put in count for something beyond a single application.</p>
