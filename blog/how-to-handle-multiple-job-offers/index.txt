3:T113e,<p>Having two offers at once is a good problem — and a stressful one. Timelines rarely line up, one company is pushing you to sign while you&#39;re still waiting on another, and it&#39;s easy to make a rushed decision you regret. Here&#39;s how to manage competing offers with a clear head, honestly and without torching relationships you might need again.</p>
<h2>Compare total value, not just base salary</h2>
<p>Base salary is the loudest number and rarely the most important one. Build a simple comparison across what actually shapes your day and your finances:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Total comp:</strong> base + bonus + equity (and what that equity realistically means — early-stage options are a lottery ticket, not cash).</li>
<li><strong>The work itself:</strong> what you&#39;ll build, the tech, the ownership you&#39;ll have.</li>
<li><strong>The people:</strong> your manager and teammates. This predicts your happiness more than almost anything.</li>
<li><strong>Growth:</strong> will you learn and level up, or plateau?</li>
<li><strong>Logistics:</strong> remote/hybrid, hours, commute, PTO, health coverage.</li>
<li><strong>Stability:</strong> runway and trajectory of the company.</li>
</ul>
<p>Write it down. Seeing it on one page cuts through the anxiety and the recency bias of whichever recruiter called last.</p>
<h2>Buy time gracefully</h2>
<p>You almost always have more time than you think. When an offer arrives, it&#39;s completely normal to say:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>&quot;Thank you — I&#39;m genuinely excited about this. This is an important decision and I&#39;d like a few days to consider it properly. Could I get back to you by [date]?&quot;</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Most companies grant a reasonable window (often up to a week). If one refuses to give you even a couple of days, that pressure is itself data about how they operate.</p>
<h2>Tell the other company you have an offer</h2>
<p>If you&#39;re waiting on a company you prefer, let them know — politely and factually:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>&quot;I want to be transparent: I&#39;ve received an offer with a decision deadline of [date]. You&#39;re my top choice, so I&#39;d love to know if it&#39;s possible to expedite your process.&quot;</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This isn&#39;t a threat; it&#39;s information that helps them help you. It often accelerates a stalled pipeline, and a company that wants you will usually try to move.</p>
<h2>Using leverage without being a jerk</h2>
<p>Competing offers are legitimate, powerful leverage for negotiation — used with grace. Keep it honest:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Never invent an offer.</strong> If a company asks for details and yours is fictional, you&#39;ve lost all trust.</li>
<li><strong>Frame it collaboratively:</strong> &quot;I&#39;d love to join — is there flexibility to close the gap with the other offer I&#39;m holding?&quot; beats &quot;match this or I walk.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Negotiate the offer you want most</strong>, using the other as a genuine, factual benchmark.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Watch for the wrong reasons</h2>
<p>Under deadline pressure, it&#39;s easy to over-weight the shiny stuff:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A slightly higher salary</strong> rarely compensates for a manager you didn&#39;t click with or work that bores you.</li>
<li><strong>Brand names</strong> feel safe but can mean a narrow role and slow growth.</li>
<li><strong>The fear of &quot;wasting&quot; the other offer</strong> is not a reason to take a job. A declined offer costs you nothing.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Decline the others cleanly</h2>
<p>Once you decide, tell the companies you&#39;re turning down — promptly, warmly, and without over-explaining:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>&quot;Thank you so much for the offer and the time your team invested. After a lot of thought I&#39;ve decided to accept another opportunity that&#39;s a closer fit right now. I really enjoyed meeting everyone and hope our paths cross again.&quot;</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The tech world is small. The recruiter you decline gracefully today may be hiring for your dream role in two years — and will remember that you were a class act.</p>
<h2>Then commit</h2>
<p>Once you&#39;ve signed, stop second-guessing. There&#39;s rarely a single &quot;correct&quot; choice, and endlessly re-litigating it only steals your energy from the new role. You gathered the facts, weighed what mattered, and chose. Go do great work.</p>
