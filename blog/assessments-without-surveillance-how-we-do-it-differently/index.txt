3:T14a3,<p>Most assessment platforms protect test integrity by surveilling the candidate: webcam monitoring, screen recording, lockdown browsers, eye-tracking, &quot;suspicious behavior&quot; flags. We deliberately don&#39;t. Not because we don&#39;t care about integrity — we care about it enormously — but because surveillance is both hostile to the strong candidates you want <em>and</em>, quietly, not very effective. Here&#39;s the philosophy behind how we built assessments, and why it gives you better signal.</p>
<h2>Surveillance is theater</h2>
<p>Here&#39;s the uncomfortable truth the proctoring industry doesn&#39;t advertise: the controls that claim to stop cheating are defeated by a second device. A candidate determined to look something up can simply use their phone, off-camera. Webcam proctoring can&#39;t stop that — so what does it actually do? It:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Insults your best candidates</strong>, who read &quot;we assume you&#39;re a cheat&quot; and take the offer that respects them instead.</li>
<li><strong>Punishes the anxious and the neurodivergent</strong>, whose normal behavior gets flagged as &quot;suspicious.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Creates false positives</strong> that put you in the ugly position of accusing someone based on a flaky algorithm.</li>
<li><strong>Collects sensitive biometric data</strong> you now have to store and protect.</li>
</ul>
<p>It&#39;s security theater that damages your brand and your candidate pipeline while providing a false sense of safety.</p>
<h2>Integrity by design instead</h2>
<p>We move integrity to where it actually works: the server and the content. You get trustworthy results without watching anyone. Concretely:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Answer keys never leave the server.</strong> Grading data lives in columns that are never sent on any candidate-facing path. There&#39;s nothing in the network traffic or page bundle to read.</li>
<li><strong>Per-candidate question sets.</strong> Each candidate draws a materialized set from a bank several times larger than the draw, with question and option order shuffled. Two candidates rarely see the same test, so sharing answers doesn&#39;t work well.</li>
<li><strong>The set is fixed at start.</strong> Restarting or refreshing resumes the <em>same</em> questions — there&#39;s no rerolling for an easier draw.</li>
<li><strong>Server-authoritative timing.</strong> The clock is owned by the server and written once when the attempt begins. No client-side timestamp is trusted for scoring.</li>
<li><strong>One-time invite links.</strong> Each invitation is a single-use token, stored only as a hash, bound to the invited person and expiring. A forwarded link doesn&#39;t create a second, cleaner attempt.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of this requires a camera. All of it is harder to defeat than proctoring — and invisible to the candidate, who just has a calm, focused experience.</p>
<h2>We&#39;re honest about what we don&#39;t control</h2>
<p>We won&#39;t pretend to do the impossible. A candidate <em>can</em> open an AI assistant in another window, and no non-surveillance control fully prevents that (nor does surveillance, given a second device). So instead of theater, we mitigate through <strong>content design</strong>: questions that test judgment, reasoning, and the ability to read and diagnose real code — not trivia an assistant instantly answers. The goal is questions where genuine understanding shows, and where a copy-pasted answer doesn&#39;t reliably win. That&#39;s a more honest and more durable defense than a webcam.</p>
<h2>Signals, not verdicts</h2>
<p>We do collect lightweight, neutral integrity metadata — things like whether the tab lost focus. But we treat it exactly as what it is: <strong>soft context, never a verdict.</strong> These signals:</p>
<ul>
<li>Are presented to you as <strong>plain, neutral facts</strong>, separate from the score.</li>
<li><strong>Never automatically fail a candidate.</strong> A person might switch tabs to grab a glass of water; the system doesn&#39;t get to decide that&#39;s cheating.</li>
<li>Are <strong>best-effort</strong> — losing a telemetry event never blocks a candidate&#39;s answer from saving.</li>
</ul>
<p>You stay in control of interpretation. We just give you honest context alongside a real score, and let you use judgment.</p>
<h2>Why this produces better hiring signal</h2>
<p>The candidates who react worst to surveillance are disproportionately the senior, in-demand people you most want to hire — the ones with other offers. A respectful, non-hostile assessment keeps them in your funnel instead of driving them out of it. And because our integrity lives in the architecture rather than in intimidation, the scores you get are trustworthy without a single accusation. You get the signal <em>and</em> the goodwill.</p>
<h2>The principle</h2>
<p>We think testing candidates and respecting candidates are not opposites — that the industry made them feel opposite by leaning on surveillance. Build integrity into the server and the content, present signals as context rather than judgment, and you can measure ability honestly while treating every candidate like an adult. That&#39;s the difference in how we do assessments, and it&#39;s a deliberate one.</p>
