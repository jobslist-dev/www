3:T113d,<p>If you&#39;ve taken an online assessment before, you may have been asked to turn on your webcam, share your screen, install a lockdown browser, or sit still while an algorithm decided whether your eye movements looked &quot;suspicious.&quot; We don&#39;t do any of that, on purpose. This article explains what our assessments do and don&#39;t collect, and why we&#39;re confident we can keep things fair without watching you.</p>
<h2>What we don&#39;t do</h2>
<p>Let&#39;s be direct about what you will <em>never</em> encounter in a JobsList.dev assessment:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No webcam monitoring.</strong> We don&#39;t look at you, record you, or analyze your face or eyes.</li>
<li><strong>No screen recording or screen sharing.</strong></li>
<li><strong>No lockdown or &quot;secure&quot; browser</strong> that takes over your machine.</li>
<li><strong>No threatening warnings</strong> about being watched or about switching tabs.</li>
<li><strong>No biometric data collection.</strong> We don&#39;t want it and we don&#39;t store it.</li>
</ul>
<p>You take the assessment in your own space, on your own device, comfortably.</p>
<h2>What we do collect — and only this</h2>
<p>We practice data minimization. For an assessment, we keep:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your <strong>email and name</strong> (so the company knows whose result it is).</li>
<li>Your <strong>answers</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Timing</strong> information (so the time limit is fair).</li>
<li>Lightweight, neutral notes such as whether the browser tab lost focus.</li>
</ul>
<p>That&#39;s the list. We&#39;re not building a profile of your behavior or your face.</p>
<h2>Why we refuse to surveil</h2>
<p>Two reasons, and both matter.</p>
<p><strong>First, it&#39;s disrespectful.</strong> Watching a professional through their webcam to make sure they don&#39;t cheat assumes the worst about them from the start. That&#39;s not how we want to treat the people companies are hoping to hire, and it&#39;s not the tone we want our platform to set.</p>
<p><strong>Second, it doesn&#39;t even work.</strong> The honest truth the proctoring industry avoids: anyone determined to look something up can use their phone, off-camera. Surveillance can&#39;t stop that — so it delivers a false sense of security while making the experience hostile for everyone, especially people who are anxious, neurodivergent, or simply in a shared living space. It flags normal behavior as &quot;suspicious&quot; and creates false accusations. We&#39;d rather not participate in security theater.</p>
<h2>So how is it fair without watching me?</h2>
<p>Fairness lives in the design, not in surveillance. Behind the scenes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Everyone gets a personalized set of questions</strong>, drawn from a much larger pool and shuffled, so the test isn&#39;t something you can pass by getting answers from a friend.</li>
<li><strong>The correct answers never reach your browser</strong> — there&#39;s nothing to &quot;find&quot; in the page or network traffic even if you went looking.</li>
<li><strong>The timer is handled fairly and consistently</strong> for everyone, on our side.</li>
<li><strong>The questions reward understanding</strong>, not memorization — they&#39;re about judgment and reasoning, which is hard to shortcut and doesn&#39;t depend on us spying on you.</li>
</ul>
<p>You get a fair test; we get trustworthy results; nobody gets watched.</p>
<h2>About those &quot;tab focus&quot; notes</h2>
<p>We do note simple, neutral things like whether your browser tab lost focus during the assessment. Please don&#39;t stress about it. This is <strong>context for the company, never an automatic judgment.</strong> People switch tabs to grab water, answer a message, or check the time — the system does not decide that&#39;s cheating, and it will never fail you on its own. It&#39;s shown to reviewers as a plain fact, and a human uses their judgment. We&#39;d rather be honest that we note it than pretend we don&#39;t.</p>
<h2>The trust goes both ways</h2>
<p>We ask you to do your own work, and in return we commit to treating you like a professional: no cameras, no intimidation, no collecting things we don&#39;t need. We think that&#39;s the relationship a good assessment should start from — and it&#39;s a deliberate choice, not an oversight. Take a breath, do your best, and know that we&#39;re not watching over your shoulder.</p>
