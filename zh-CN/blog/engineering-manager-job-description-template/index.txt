e:T3a45,<p>Hiring an engineering manager is one of the highest-leverage decisions a technology company can make.</p>
<p>A great manager multiplies the effectiveness of an entire engineering team. They help developers grow, improve technical execution, remove organizational friction, strengthen hiring, and create an environment where talented people want to stay.</p>
<p>A poor manager can have the opposite effect—high turnover, slower delivery, frustrated engineers, unclear priorities, and declining product quality.</p>
<p>Unfortunately, many Engineering Manager job descriptions are vague. They ask candidates to &quot;lead high-performing teams&quot; without explaining what success actually looks like.</p>
<p>Experienced engineering managers look for answers to questions that many job posts never address:</p>
<ul>
<li>How many engineers will I manage?</li>
<li>Will I still write code?</li>
<li>How much authority will I actually have?</li>
<li>Am I inheriting a healthy team or rebuilding one?</li>
<li>Who owns technical direction?</li>
<li>Who sets priorities?</li>
<li>How is success measured?</li>
<li>Will I be expected to hire?</li>
<li>Is this primarily people management or delivery management?</li>
</ul>
<p>Specificity attracts experienced leaders.</p>
<p>Ambiguity attracts applicants hoping to figure it out later.</p>
<p>This guide includes a practical Engineering Manager job description template, along with interview guidance and screening tips that help companies identify exceptional technical leaders.</p>
<hr>
<h1>What Does an Engineering Manager Actually Do?</h1>
<p>Engineering Managers exist to improve the performance of an engineering organization.</p>
<p>They accomplish this through people rather than through individual code contributions.</p>
<p>A successful Engineering Manager balances several responsibilities simultaneously:</p>
<ul>
<li>Hiring exceptional engineers</li>
<li>Coaching and mentoring team members</li>
<li>Removing organizational blockers</li>
<li>Partnering with Product and Design</li>
<li>Improving engineering processes</li>
<li>Building healthy team culture</li>
<li>Delivering software predictably</li>
<li>Supporting career development</li>
<li>Managing performance fairly</li>
<li>Helping engineers do their best work</li>
</ul>
<p>The role becomes increasingly strategic as organizations grow.</p>
<hr>
<h1>Engineering Manager Job Description Template</h1>
<h2>Engineering Manager</h2>
<p><strong>Location:</strong> Remote / Hybrid / On-site</p>
<p><strong>Employment Type:</strong> Full-Time</p>
<p><strong>Compensation:</strong> [$X–$Y base + bonus + equity]</p>
<p><strong>Reports To:</strong> Director of Engineering / VP Engineering / CTO</p>
<hr>
<h2>About the Role</h2>
<p>At <strong>[Company]</strong>, we build software that helps <strong>[customers]</strong> accomplish <strong>[mission]</strong>.</p>
<p>Our engineering organization consists of <strong>[number] engineers</strong> across <strong>[number] teams</strong>, working on products that serve <strong>[customers or users]</strong>.</p>
<p>We&#39;re looking for an Engineering Manager to lead the <strong>[team name]</strong>, responsible for <strong>[product area or technical domain]</strong>.</p>
<p>Today the team includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Senior Engineers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Mid-Level Engineers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Junior Engineers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Staff Engineers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> QA Engineers</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> DevOps Engineers</li>
</ul>
<p>Please describe the current situation honestly.</p>
<p>Examples include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The team is growing rapidly.</li>
<li>We&#39;re building a brand-new product.</li>
<li>We&#39;re modernizing an existing platform.</li>
<li>The team recently expanded.</li>
<li>We&#39;re replacing a departing manager.</li>
<li>We&#39;re improving engineering practices across multiple teams.</li>
</ul>
<p>Experienced managers appreciate transparency.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What You&#39;ll Do</h2>
<ul>
<li>Lead, coach, and mentor a team of software engineers.</li>
<li>Conduct regular one-on-one meetings and performance reviews.</li>
<li>Support career growth through coaching, feedback, and development planning.</li>
<li>Partner with Product Managers and Designers to prioritize engineering work.</li>
<li>Improve planning, estimation, and delivery predictability.</li>
<li>Help establish healthy engineering processes and technical standards.</li>
<li>Remove blockers that slow the team down.</li>
<li>Participate in hiring, interviewing, and onboarding engineers.</li>
<li>Collaborate with senior technical leaders on architectural decisions.</li>
<li>Foster psychological safety, collaboration, and continuous improvement.</li>
<li>Communicate project status, risks, and trade-offs to stakeholders.</li>
<li>Help engineers grow into senior technical leaders.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Coding Expectations</h2>
<p>One of the most important sections in the job posting.</p>
<p>State it explicitly.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<p><strong>Hands-on (20%)</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Code reviews</li>
<li>Architecture discussions</li>
<li>Small bug fixes</li>
<li>Technical spikes</li>
<li>Internal tooling</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<p><strong>Player-Coach (50%)</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Active feature development</li>
<li>Mentoring</li>
<li>Delivery ownership</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<p><strong>Management-Focused (0–10%)</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Leadership</li>
<li>Strategy</li>
<li>Hiring</li>
<li>Organizational planning</li>
</ul>
<p>Candidates self-select heavily based on this expectation.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Required Qualifications</h2>
<ul>
<li>X+ years of professional software engineering experience.</li>
<li>X+ years managing software engineers.</li>
<li>Experience leading performance reviews and career development.</li>
<li>Strong technical background with modern software architecture.</li>
<li>Experience hiring and interviewing engineers.</li>
<li>Excellent written and verbal communication skills.</li>
<li>Ability to prioritize competing business needs.</li>
<li>Experience collaborating with Product and Design leadership.</li>
<li>Proven ability to build healthy engineering culture.</li>
<li>Experience leading projects from planning through production.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Nice to Have</h2>
<ul>
<li>Experience managing remote teams.</li>
<li>Startup experience.</li>
<li>Enterprise software experience.</li>
<li>Cloud infrastructure knowledge.</li>
<li>Agile coaching experience.</li>
<li>Scaling engineering organizations.</li>
<li>Managing managers.</li>
<li>Organizational change leadership.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Team Structure</h2>
<p>Provide concrete information.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<p>Engineering Organization:</p>
<ul>
<li>VP Engineering</li>
<li>Directors</li>
<li>Engineering Managers</li>
<li>Staff Engineers</li>
<li>Senior Engineers</li>
<li>Software Engineers</li>
<li>QA</li>
<li>Platform</li>
</ul>
<p>Candidates want to understand how decisions are made.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Decision-Making Authority</h2>
<p>Be honest about what this role owns.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<p>Responsible for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Hiring decisions</li>
<li>Performance reviews</li>
<li>Promotions</li>
<li>Sprint planning</li>
<li>Team processes</li>
<li>Delivery execution</li>
<li>Team staffing</li>
</ul>
<p>Shared ownership:</p>
<ul>
<li>Technical architecture</li>
<li>Roadmaps</li>
<li>Prioritization</li>
<li>Hiring standards</li>
</ul>
<p>Owned elsewhere:</p>
<ul>
<li>Company budgets</li>
<li>Executive strategy</li>
<li>Compensation bands</li>
<li>Organization-wide headcount</li>
</ul>
<p>Managers without meaningful authority often become frustrated.</p>
<p>Clarify expectations early.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Success in the First 90 Days</h2>
<p>Strong candidates want to know what success looks like.</p>
<p>Example goals:</p>
<p>First 30 days</p>
<ul>
<li>Build relationships</li>
<li>Learn the product</li>
<li>Understand the architecture</li>
<li>Meet stakeholders</li>
</ul>
<p>Days 31–60</p>
<ul>
<li>Improve planning</li>
<li>Remove bottlenecks</li>
<li>Begin coaching</li>
<li>Support hiring</li>
</ul>
<p>Days 61–90</p>
<ul>
<li>Increase delivery confidence</li>
<li>Improve team health</li>
<li>Establish regular feedback</li>
<li>Ship meaningful improvements</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2>Interview Process</h2>
<h3>Stage 1</h3>
<p>Recruiter or hiring manager conversation.</p>
<p>Discuss expectations, team, and organizational context.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Stage 2</h3>
<p>Engineering leadership interview.</p>
<p>Topics include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Coaching</li>
<li>Hiring</li>
<li>Conflict resolution</li>
<li>Performance management</li>
<li>Career development</li>
<li>Cross-functional collaboration</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h3>Stage 3</h3>
<p>Technical discussion.</p>
<p>Rather than solving algorithm puzzles, discuss:</p>
<ul>
<li>System design decisions</li>
<li>Architecture trade-offs</li>
<li>Technical leadership</li>
<li>Delivery planning</li>
<li>Engineering judgment</li>
</ul>
<p>Engineering Managers should maintain technical credibility even if they no longer code daily.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Stage 4</h3>
<p>Meet the engineering team.</p>
<p>Candidates should spend time with future direct reports.</p>
<p>This often reveals mutual fit better than executive interviews.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Stage 5</h3>
<p>Cross-functional interview.</p>
<p>Meet:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product</li>
<li>Design</li>
<li>Security</li>
<li>Infrastructure</li>
<li>Executive leadership</li>
</ul>
<p>Healthy partnerships are essential.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Benefits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Competitive salary</li>
<li>Equity</li>
<li>Health benefits</li>
<li>Remote flexibility</li>
<li>Home office stipend</li>
<li>Learning budget</li>
<li>Conference attendance</li>
<li>Paid parental leave</li>
<li>Flexible PTO</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1>Hiring Engineering Managers Successfully</h1>
<h2>Publish the Three Numbers Candidates Care About</h2>
<p>Nearly every experienced manager wants to know:</p>
<ul>
<li>How many direct reports?</li>
<li>How much coding?</li>
<li>How much hiring?</li>
</ul>
<p>Leaving these unanswered creates unnecessary uncertainty.</p>
<hr>
<h1>Be Honest About Team Health</h1>
<p>Managers appreciate transparency.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<p>&quot;We&#39;re growing from one team to three.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;Our delivery process needs improvement.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;The team recently experienced significant growth.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;We&#39;re modernizing an older platform.&quot;</p>
<p>&quot;This role will help rebuild engineering culture.&quot;</p>
<p>Strong managers often enjoy difficult challenges.</p>
<p>They dislike unpleasant surprises.</p>
<hr>
<h1>Look for Multipliers</h1>
<p>The best Engineering Managers improve everyone around them.</p>
<p>Ask yourself:</p>
<p>Does this person make other engineers stronger?</p>
<p>Do they create clarity?</p>
<p>Can they coach effectively?</p>
<p>Can they hire exceptional people?</p>
<p>Do they improve delivery without creating burnout?</p>
<p>The answers matter far more than whether they can still reverse a binary tree on a whiteboard.</p>
<hr>
<h1>Interview for Real Leadership Stories</h1>
<p>Behavioral interviews are particularly valuable for management roles.</p>
<p>Ask questions like:</p>
<h3>Tell me about an engineer who struggled.</h3>
<p>How did you identify the problem?</p>
<p>How did you coach them?</p>
<p>What changed?</p>
<p>What did you learn?</p>
<hr>
<h3>Tell me about someone you promoted.</h3>
<p>Why were they promoted?</p>
<p>How did you help them grow?</p>
<p>What opportunities did you create?</p>
<hr>
<h3>Describe your most difficult hiring decision.</h3>
<p>Why was it difficult?</p>
<p>What trade-offs did you make?</p>
<p>Would you make the same decision today?</p>
<hr>
<h3>Tell me about a project that missed its deadline.</h3>
<p>What happened?</p>
<p>How did you communicate it?</p>
<p>What process improvements came afterward?</p>
<hr>
<h3>Describe a disagreement with senior leadership.</h3>
<p>How did you advocate for your team?</p>
<p>What compromise was reached?</p>
<hr>
<h3>Tell me about a time you had to say &quot;no.&quot;</h3>
<p>Engineering Managers protect their teams.</p>
<p>Look for candidates who can negotiate priorities respectfully rather than simply accepting every request.</p>
<hr>
<h3>What engineering culture are you most proud of building?</h3>
<p>This question often reveals:</p>
<ul>
<li>coaching philosophy</li>
<li>leadership style</li>
<li>hiring philosophy</li>
<li>communication skills</li>
<li>emotional intelligence</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1>Evaluate Leadership, Not Buzzwords</h1>
<p>Avoid candidates who rely entirely on management terminology without concrete examples.</p>
<p>Phrases like:</p>
<ul>
<li>Servant leadership</li>
<li>Radical candor</li>
<li>Ownership</li>
<li>Empowerment</li>
<li>Growth mindset</li>
</ul>
<p>are useful only when backed by real experiences.</p>
<p>Ask for stories.</p>
<p>Specific examples are far more valuable than polished vocabulary.</p>
<hr>
<h1>Common Hiring Mistakes</h1>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<ul>
<li>Never explaining coding expectations.</li>
<li>Hiding organizational challenges.</li>
<li>Measuring managers only by delivery speed.</li>
<li>Ignoring coaching ability.</li>
<li>Treating management as senior engineering plus meetings.</li>
<li>Failing to let the team meet candidates.</li>
<li>Giving responsibility without authority.</li>
<li>Overlooking emotional intelligence and communication.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1>Qualities of Exceptional Engineering Managers</h1>
<p>The strongest managers consistently demonstrate:</p>
<ul>
<li>Technical credibility</li>
<li>Excellent communication</li>
<li>Coaching ability</li>
<li>Hiring judgment</li>
<li>Emotional intelligence</li>
<li>Clear prioritization</li>
<li>Organizational thinking</li>
<li>Calm under pressure</li>
<li>Strong decision-making</li>
<li>Accountability</li>
<li>Empathy</li>
<li>Integrity</li>
</ul>
<p>They create environments where engineers can do their best work.</p>
<hr>
<h1>The Takeaway</h1>
<p>Hiring an Engineering Manager is ultimately about finding someone who makes an entire team more successful.</p>
<p>Be transparent about the size and health of the team, clearly define coding expectations, explain what authority the role carries, and interview for concrete leadership experiences rather than management buzzwords.</p>
<p>Exceptional engineering managers don&#39;t simply deliver projects—they develop people, strengthen engineering culture, improve execution, and help organizations build better software for years to come.</p>
